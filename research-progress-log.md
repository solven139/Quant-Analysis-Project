# Cross-Asset Trend-Following Research Project — Progress Log

*Last updated: 2026-09-27 (Phase E — Pillar 3 capital allocation, EW/IVP/HRP/CLA-GMV, real-data result + significance test complete)*

## Project Summary

Testing whether a simple, well-documented trading rule holds up under statistically rigorous scrutiny, can be risk-managed effectively, and can be allocated capital sensibly. The project deliberately does not attempt to forecast returns from scratch. It's organized around three pillars, deliberately kept strategy-agnostic so a new primary rule can be dropped in without rebuilding the framework:

1. **Is the edge statistically real?** (Permutation Test + Deflated Sharpe Ratio)
2. **How is risk controlled around it?** (Volatility Targeting + Meta-Labeling)
3. **How is capital allocated across it?** (Equal Weight vs. Inverse Vol vs. Risk Parity vs. Hierarchical Risk Parity)

**Universe (expanded, current):** 14 liquid ETFs spanning equities, bonds, commodities, and real assets — the original 4 plus a 10-ticker expansion added specifically to give Pillar 1's significance tests (and later Pillar 3's HRP comparison) a meaningfully larger cross-section to work with. See "Phase 0b — Universe Expansion" below.

**Primary rule tested through Phase 3b (concluded):** sign of trailing N-month total return (time-series momentum). Long if positive, short if negative (R_t(N) exactly 0 treated as long — a pre-committed tie-break). N tested across a pre-committed grid: 3, 6, 12 months, in parallel. Signal evaluated at month-end on daily closing data; a position signaled at month-end t executes on the *next* trading day, held constant until the next month-end signal. **Headline finding: no statistically robust edge, unconditional or conditional, that this project's testing could detect** (Phase 6a/6b unconditional null; two independent meta-labeling rounds, seven features total, also null). See "Phase 3b Conclusion" below.

**Second primary rule tested (concluded): statistical arbitrage / pairs trading**, via Engle-Granger cointegration screening across the same 14-asset universe. Its screen and multiple-testing corrections ran on real data and flagged one apparent standout pair (Phase 2/2b), but identifying that pair by name revealed it was a **one-off COVID-crash-window artifact, not genuine structural cointegration** (see "Phase 3a Pre-Check" below) — a finding that prompted a design correction: a methodologically standard walk-forward version of the rule (select-and-trade a portfolio of whatever pairs clear a point-in-time, within-window FDR correction each period, never a single globally-best pair). Run on real data, it produced a descriptive Sharpe of 0.207 and cumulative return of 34.0% — but a window-level P&L decomposition found that result **heavily concentrated in the same kind of crisis-driven artifact already diagnosed at the screening stage** (one window contributes 60% of the entire cumulative return; another selected 16 pairs simultaneously right after the COVID formation window and lost money). Excluding those two windows looked cleaner, but doing so *after* seeing which one won and lost would itself be data snooping — so the honest next step was a full-pipeline Monte Carlo permutation test of the complete, as-built strategy (see "Phase 6a (Pairs Rule)" below). **That real test came back null: MC p-value = 0.3775 for both Sharpe and cumulative return — indistinguishable from a chance-alone null of the exact same search-and-select procedure.** Combined with the screening-level and portfolio-level crisis-artifact diagnoses that preceded it, **statistical-arbitrage pairs trading on this 14-asset universe, as designed here, does not survive disciplined testing** — a second primary rule concluded null, via a different route than momentum's.

**Third primary rule tested (concluded): Bollinger Bands mean-reversion** (single-asset, per-asset independent), chosen over RSI and over continuing to search within pairs-trading designs specifically because it reuses the shape of machinery already validated for pairs trading's spread rule. Run on real data, the descriptive result was **decisively negative** — the strategy beat buy-and-hold on Sharpe in **0 of 14 assets**, with **7 of 14 assets showing outright negative raw strategy Sharpe** and **all 14 assets more short-days than long-days** — consistent with the naive two-sided contrarian rule fighting each asset's own secular uptrend, chiefly via the short leg (all 14 assets have substantial positive buy-and-hold returns over their tradeable histories). Because a negative-to-near-zero raw Sharpe on half the universe cannot become significant under a permutation test of the same rule, formal MCPT/DSR testing was judged to be a formality rather than a real open question, and — by explicit user decision — was skipped. **Bollinger Bands is concluded null without a formal significance-testing pass**, a third consecutive null primary rule on this universe, arrived at via a third distinct route (an outright negative raw effect, rather than a chance-level positive one). See "Phase 3a (Bollinger Bands Rule) — Real-Data Result & Conclusion" and "Strategic Decision — Declaring Bollinger Bands Concluded Null" below.

**Fourth primary rule tested (concluded): seasonality / calendar effects**, specifically the **month-end effect** (long the last 2 + first 4 trading days of each calendar month, long-only). Chosen deliberately as the most different available hypothesis from the first three — it is the only candidate that trades entirely on the calendar, with no dependence on any asset's own price history at all, after a user-requested reading of Kaufman's *Trading Systems and Methods* surfaced several non-price-based candidates (seasonality, volume/breadth indicators, carry trade, the COT report, multiple time frames). The real-data descriptive result was genuinely mixed, not decisive either way — a naive "beats buy-and-hold in 1 of 14 assets" headline was itself found to be a somewhat misleading read, given this rule is only ~28.6% exposed to the market by construction (see "Phase 3a (Seasonality Rule)" below for why that changes what a fair comparison looks like). Because the raw result didn't foreclose the question the way Bollinger Bands' did, a dedicated Monte Carlo permutation test was built and run (**0/14 assets significant at p<0.05 on either Sharpe or cumulative return; the best case, EEM, only reaches p≈0.10-0.12 — unremarkable given 14 simultaneous tests**). **Seasonality (month-end effect) is concluded null** — a fourth consecutive null primary rule, and notably the one hypothesis this project judged least likely to share a common failure mode with the first three, also failing. See "Phase 2/3a/6 (Seasonality Rule)" below. This session also uncovered and resolved a data-integrity incident (the real WRDS panel was missing from a fresh session and nearly replaced by leftover synthetic test-fixture data) — see "Data Integrity Incident" below; it does not affect any of the three prior conclusions, which are confirmed to have run on genuinely real data. **Decided: move to Phase 9** rather than search for a fifth primary rule — see "Next Step" for the revisited candidate ranking that informed this, and the [Phase 9 Audit & Findings doc](https://claude.ai/code/artifact/3d4f78f3-cce2-4718-b9a8-d4e82887027f) for the full writeup.

**Post-Phase-9 continuation (in progress):** despite Phase 9 formally closing the four-rule search, the user asked to continue exploring — first prompted by a review of their own reference code (a lean single-asset MCPT pipeline, and a separate AFML-based meta-labeling notebook), which led to a deliberate simplification: single-asset rules with no fitted parameter need only one direct MCPT pass, not the full search-correction-DSR machinery this project built for momentum. **A fifth test, single-asset TLT carry trade (the Treasury term-spread signal), also came back null** (real Sharpe 0.350 vs. buy-and-hold 0.328, MC p=0.229 — not significant), and extending it to IEF/SHY surfaced a genuine, unplanned finding: the carry mechanism **reverses sign by bond duration** (bad for long-duration TLT when the curve inverts, but actually favorable for short-duration SHY), which rules out simply pooling the three assets for more statistical power. This prompted a further pivot — testing a genuinely different AFML *data type* (fundamental data) rather than another price-derived signal — via a **Dow-30 book-to-market value factor**, which required leaving the project's 14-ETF universe for individual stocks (by explicit user decision) and a new WRDS pull (Compustat + CRSP). The value factor's full signal/backtest/MCPT pipeline is built and validated on synthetic data; all 30 Dow tickers are now resolved to CRSP permnos, a raw-price-vs-total-return design gap was found and fixed before any real data was touched, and the merge script that will turn the raw WRDS pull into the pipeline's tidy inputs is built and validated; the real-data run awaits the user's refreshed, full 30-ticker WRDS pull. See "Phase 10" onward below.

**Sixth primary rule tested (concluded): trend-filtered mean-reversion**, an original strategy designed from scratch specifically to have risk management built in from the start, rather than retrofitted onto an already-null rule. It combines a 252-day trend filter (only take mean-reversion trades in the direction of the longer-term trend — directly targeting Bollinger Bands' own diagnosed failure mode of shorting into secular uptrends) with a full risk-management overlay (stop-loss/take-profit, volatility-based position sizing, a portfolio-level drawdown circuit breaker). Run on the real 14-asset panel with a train/validate split, the strategy showed a genuinely encouraging Monte Carlo permutation test result — 5 of 14 assets significant at p<0.05 on Sharpe (EEM, EFA, HYG, IWM, SPY), the first time since momentum that more than one asset has cleared that bar — but the mandatory Deflated Sharpe Ratio follow-up came back decisively negative: **0 of 14 assets survive DSR at the 0.95 threshold.** The dispersion in Sharpe ratios across the 14 trial assets inflates the multiple-testing-corrected benchmark (SR0) enough that even SPY's strong-looking result doesn't clear it, and heavy kurtosis in the strategy's daily returns (37–207 across assets, vs. Gaussian's 3.0) further penalizes the Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio. **Trend-filtered mean-reversion is concluded null under the project's full two-part standard (MCPT-and-DSR)** — a sixth consecutive null primary rule, and the most methodologically instructive one yet, since a naive stop at the MCPT stage alone would have wrongly suggested a real discovery. Along the way, a genuine bug was found and fixed in the risk overlay's own drawdown circuit breaker (it was deciding its on/off state from realized, breaker-cut equity, which traps it permanently on after a single trigger — fixed via a "shadow equity" design that decides from the hypothetical uninterrupted path instead). See "Phase 12" below.

**Options-hedge overlay tested (concluded — a genuine risk/return trade-off, not a null result): a 25-delta, 30-day protective put, rolled monthly, on the plain passive 14-asset equal-weight buy-and-hold portfolio** (not on any of the six null primary rules, since hedging a signal with no validated edge wouldn't be a meaningful test). Unlike every phase above, this was never a test of whether an edge is statistically real — a hedge is not claimed to generate alpha, so no MCPT/DSR follow-up applies — but a direct, real-data comparison of hedged vs. unhedged risk and return over the ~26.5-year window common to all 14 assets' real options data (1999-2025). The put overlay behaves exactly as insurance theory predicts: it meaningfully reduces drawdown and tail risk, most dramatically in a fast, V-shaped shock (2020 COVID: max drawdown cut from 21.4% to 7.8%, finishing the window with a *better* cumulative return than unhedged), more modestly in a slower-moving decline (2008 GFC: 34.0%→22.2%; 2022 rate-hike selloff: 19.6%→16.3%) — but the continuous cost of the monthly premium is a severe drag on long-run compounded wealth: cumulative return over the full matched window falls from 346.8% (unhedged) to 58.4% (hedged), and annualized Sharpe actually falls (0.40→0.20) despite the lower drawdown — a well-documented real-world property of continuously-held protective-put strategies (mirrored by CBOE's own PPUT index). Two genuine data bugs were found and fixed before trusting the result: a stray, ticker-reused secid in the WRDS OptionMetrics pull (resolved via cusip mismatch, the same class of issue as Phase 11's CRSP ticker reuse), and — more consequentially — CRSP's raw price series not being split-adjusted, which made six real stock splits across the universe look like instantaneous 60-90% crashes to the overlay's fixed-dollar strike tracking until fixed. See "Phase C, Part 3" below.

## Phase 6a — Monte Carlo Permutation Test (Complete)

**Purpose:** correct for *within-asset* selection bias — searching 3 lookbacks per asset and naturally reporting whichever looked best. The original 4-asset Phase 3a's GLD result (momentum Sharpe swinging from 0.11 to 0.49 to 0.40 just by changing N) was the live illustration of this risk.

**Design:** adapted directly from Timothy Masters' `MCPT_BARS.CPP` (user-supplied), generalized from Masters' long-only single-parameter design to a long/short, three-lookback design. Permute each asset's daily log returns (shuffle values, keep dates fixed), then **re-run the full "search N=3,6,12, pick the best Sharpe" procedure on every permuted trial**, not just the real data, so the null distribution reflects the lookback-search process itself. Trial 0 = real data; trials 1..1999 (NREPS=2000, seed=20260924) = permutations. Decomposes into `trend_component`, `training_bias`, and `skill`.

**Validation:** reimplementation-correctness and calibration checks, plus a power check that initially failed for a *test-design* reason (i.i.d. regime signs → genuinely nothing to detect → correctly returned p=0.715); fixed with a persistent 2-state Markov regime process (p_stay=0.90), after which the retest correctly detected the structure (p=0.008, skill=+1.49) — confirming real detection power. **This tool (`step6_mcpt_permutation_test.py`) was designed to be reusable for any new primary rule, but its internal design specifically searches a lookback grid (N=3,6,12) and picks the best per trial** — this turned out to matter in practice: none of the three subsequent primary rules (pairs trading, Bollinger Bands, seasonality) could actually reuse it as-is, each needing its own dedicated permutation-test module sharing only the design principles. See "Open Methodology Watch-Items" below for the corrected, generalized lesson.

**Output:** `data/raw/mcpt_permutation_test_results.csv`.

## Phase 6b — Deflated Sharpe Ratio (Complete)

**Purpose:** correct for the *second* layer of selection bias — searching across 14 assets in addition to Phase 6a's within-asset lookback search.

**Design decision (approved by user):** DSR computed over **14 trials** — one post-MCPT best-N Sharpe per asset — not all 42 asset×lookback combinations (which would double-count the lookback-selection correction Phase 6a already applied).

**Method:** Bailey & López de Prado (2014). `expected_max_sharpe` (extreme-value theory, Euler–Mascheroni constant) sets SR0, the multiple-testing-corrected null benchmark. Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio (Lo 2002 / Mertens 2002, accounting for T, skew, kurtosis) evaluated against SR0. All PSR/DSR math kept at a single (daily) frequency throughout — annualizing Sharpe while leaving T/skew/kurtosis daily breaks the formula's nonlinear SR² term. Validated with formula sanity checks and Monte Carlo calibration of the extreme-value approximation at the actual small N=14.

**Result:** DSR/PSR evaluated for the argmax asset (SHY) and a diagnostic set (SPY, QQQ, DBC, EEM). **No economically meaningful asset survives both corrections.** SHY "survives" DSR formally, but as a near-cash, short-duration Treasury ETF, its low volatility makes a high Sharpe mechanically easy to reach without representing a meaningful, exploitable edge.

**Output:** `dsr_trial_inputs_14assets.csv`, `dsr_summary.csv`, `dsr_diagnostic_candidates.csv`. **This tool (`step6b_deflated_sharpe_ratio.py`) computes DSR across whichever N assets it's given (14 here) and doesn't depend on any per-asset internal search, so it's reusable as-is for any new primary rule** — unlike step6, it has no lookback-grid assumption baked in. ## Phase 2 (Pairs Rule) — Cointegration Pair Screening, Statistical Arbitrage

**Purpose:** the pairs rule's equivalent of Phase 2 (signal construction) — identify which pairs among the 14-asset universe have a stationary (mean-reverting) log-price spread, as the basis for a pairs-trading rule, before any backtest or significance testing is attempted.

**Design:**
- **Universe:** same 14 assets, all C(14,2) = 91 unordered pairs.
- **Engle-Granger, both directions:** OLS regression direction is not symmetric — regressing log(P_A) on log(P_B) and log(P_B) on log(P_A) generally give different residuals and different cointegration evidence. Both directions are tested per pair (91 × 2 = **182 regressions per formation window, not 91**, when the full universe is available), and the direction with the stronger (lower p-value) evidence is kept. This regression count is what gets carried into the multiple-testing correction, not the pair count.
- **Correct critical values:** uses `statsmodels.tsa.stattools.coint`, not a manual OLS regression followed by a plain ADF test on the residuals — once alpha/beta are estimated on the same sample, ordinary ADF critical values are invalid for the residual series (a common error in naive pairs-trading implementations); `coint()` applies MacKinnon's cointegration-specific critical values/p-values instead.
- **Rolling formation/trading windows** (Gatev-Goetzmann-Rouwenhorst style): default 12-month formation / 6-month trading, non-overlapping trading windows rolled forward by the trading-window length (formation windows themselves overlap by 6 months, by design). Strictly point-in-time — a formation window's screen only ever reads data inside `[formation_start, formation_end]`, never the following trading period or anything later.
- **Input:** log of Phase 0b's `tri_full_panel_14assets.csv` (total-return index), not raw close prices — dividend reinvestment is already folded in, so mechanical ex-dividend price drops (distributional artifacts, not economic moves) aren't misread as spread-breaking events. Flagged explicitly as a methodology choice, not a silent default.
- **Staggered inception**, consistent with Phase 0b's established pattern: a pair is skipped (not imputed/forward-filled) for any formation window where either asset has incomplete history — no fabricated pre-inception data.

**Validation (synthetic data, all passing):** a genuinely cointegrated synthetic pair is detected (power check); an empirical false-positive rate of ~8% over 200 independent-random-walk trials at a nominal 5% threshold (mild, expected inflation from taking the minimum p-value across two correlated directions — itself a small multiple-comparison effect, flagged rather than hidden); the both-directions selection logic verified to exactly match the lower of two independently-computed `coint()` calls on a pair with genuine direction asymmetry; a direct point-in-time leakage check (perturbing data strictly after the formation window changes nothing); a staggered-inception exclusion check; and an exact trial-count check (a clean 14-asset panel yields exactly 91 pairs tested / 182 regressions in one window). A path bug in the test harness itself (bare relative filename for the sibling source file, broke if run from a different working directory) was found and fixed after the user hit it running locally — logic unaffected, all checks still pass.

**Real-data result:** the user ran the screen on the actual `tri_full_panel_14assets.csv`: **63 rolling formation windows, 7,214 total regressions** (91 pairs × 2 directions × 63 windows, reduced from the full 11,466 by staggered inception in earlier windows) — **3,607 pair-window tests** (one hit-eligible p-value per pair per window, after taking the stronger of the two regression directions), of which **338 cleared the naive p < 0.05 threshold.**

**Correction (erratum):** an earlier version of this log compared the 338 hits against ≈361 expected-by-chance using 7,214 as the denominator — that comparison was wrong, since 7,214 double-counts both regression directions per pair-window while a "hit" is defined at the pair-window level (after direction selection), not the regression level. The correct denominator is 3,607. At 3,607 tests and a 5% threshold, pure chance alone predicts ≈180 false positives — so the observed 338 is actually *well above* naive chance, roughly 1.9×. This comparison is still not conclusive on its own (the tests are strongly correlated across overlapping rolling windows and across the two-direction min-selection), which is exactly why Phase 2b's actual corrections below — not this raw comparison — are the real answer.

**Output artifacts:** `data/raw/pairs_cointegration_screen_all.csv`, `data/raw/pairs_cointegration_screen_window_counts.csv`.

## Phase 2b (Pairs Rule) — Multiple-Testing Correction for the Cointegration Screen

**Purpose:** replace the rough chance-rate sanity check above with an actual corrected significance result for the screen, mirroring `step6_mcpt_permutation_test.py`'s already-agreed design.

**Primary design — MCPT (`step2b_pairtrade_cointegration_mcpt.py`):**
- **Permutation scheme:** each asset's own daily log-returns are independently permuted (a different random reordering per asset, per the project's standing "not a joint/paired shuffle" rule) — destroying each series' own time-series/trend structure, and with it any genuine pairwise cointegrating relationship, while exactly preserving that asset's own marginal return distribution and its own staggered-inception valid date range. Each permuted series remains a random walk, so the null correctly captures the "spurious regression between two generically-behaved but unrelated unit-root processes" risk this whole exercise exists to rule out.
- **Trial 0 = real data; trials 1..n_trials = permuted**, exact same rolling screening procedure re-run end-to-end on every trial. **Fixed ADF lag** (no AIC search) throughout, for tractability at 200+ trials — this script's own trial-0 hit count will not exactly reproduce the 338 figure from the original adaptive-lag run, which is fine; what matters is trial 0 vs. the permutation null under identical settings.
- **Two statistics, each its own Monte Carlo p-value:** `n_hits` (count of pair-window p<0.05) and `best_p_value` (single lowest p-value anywhere — a threshold-free max-statistic check).
- **Parallelized** across trials via `concurrent.futures.ProcessPoolExecutor`.

**Validation (synthetic data, all passing):** permutation exactly preserves staggered-inception NaN positions and each asset's own return multiset (genuinely reordered, not a no-op); a **power check** (one genuinely cointegrated pair embedded among unrelated assets) gets a clearly small MC p-value; a **null/calibration check** (assets correlated via a shared return factor but with NO true cointegrating relationship) gets an unremarkable MC p-value on both statistics; `n_jobs=1` vs. `n_jobs>1` give identical real-data results; and a **checkpoint/resume check** confirms that truncating an in-progress run's checkpoint file and re-running reproduces the exact same final result as an uninterrupted run (bit-for-bit on the integer statistics, float-close on the p-values, the latter only differing by an expected CSV round-trip precision artifact).

**Benchmark (directly measured, not extrapolated):** at the real 14-asset, 63-window scale with the fixed ADF lag, one full screen (~7,200 regressions) takes **~21 seconds**; 201 total screens (1 real + 200 permuted) is **~70 minutes running serially**, scaling down with more cores.

**Execution problem hit, and fixed:** the user's Colab environment struggled to complete the run. Root cause: the script's original default `n_jobs = cpu_count() - 1` evaluates to **1** on Colab's free-tier 2-vCPU instances — i.e., it was running fully serially with zero parallelism benefit, on top of Colab's shared/often-slower CPUs and its tendency to disconnect long silent cells (which would have discarded all progress). Fixed two ways: (1) default `n_jobs` changed to use the full core count rather than count-minus-one; (2) added `checkpoint_path` support — each trial's result is written to disk as it completes, and a re-run picks up only the missing trials rather than starting over, so a Colab disconnect now costs minutes, not the whole run.

**Fallback design — Phase 2b-alt, fast FDR (`step2b_alt_fdr_correction.py`):** built as an immediate, zero-compute alternative for while the MCPT is (re-)running or if it's not worth the wait. Applies Benjamini-Hochberg (via `statsmodels.stats.multitest`, not hand-rolled) directly to the p-values already saved in `pairs_cointegration_screen_all.csv` — no re-screening, runs in under a second. Weaker than the MCPT (assumes less about the actual cross-window/cross-direction correlation structure, which the ~8% vs. nominal 5% synthetic calibration gap shows is real), but a legitimate, standard correction — treat it as a fast first read, the MCPT as the more rigorous final answer. Validated on synthetic p-value arrays: rejects ~0 of 1,000 null-only p-values at FDR q=0.10, and correctly recovers essentially all of 100 genuinely small p-values injected among 900 null ones.

**Real-data results — MCPT (`step2b_pairtrade_cointegration_mcpt.py`):** completed in 3,144s (201 trials: 1 real + 200 permuted, fixed-lag ADF; the `n_jobs` fix and checkpointing both worked as intended).

## Phase 3a Pre-Check (Pairs Rule) — Identifying the Specific Pair & the COVID-Window Artifact

**Purpose:** before building any spread-trading rule around it, identify by name the specific pair(s) behind Phase 2b's significant `best_p_value` result, and sanity-check whether it represents genuine structure or a fluke — the same skepticism this project applied to SHY "surviving" DSR in the momentum phase.

**Finding:** sorting the real screen output by `p_value` ascending, the single strongest result is **HYG (dependent) regressed on SPY (independent)**: t=-6.82, p=2.296e-08, hedge_ratio=0.432, n_obs=252, formation window 2020-02-14 to 2021-02-14 — the same order of magnitude as the MCPT's fixed-lag `best_p_value` (2.131e-10; the small numeric difference is expected, since that run used a fixed ADF lag=1 vs. this screen's AIC-selected lag).

**Persistence check across all 34 windows HYG/SPY was actually tested in:** it clears p<0.05 in exactly **1 of 34** windows — and that one window is 2020-02-14 to 2021-02-14, precisely the COVID crash and V-shaped recovery. Every other window: p ranges from 0.05 to 0.93, most well above 0.3. **Verdict: disqualified.** A 12-month window containing a >30% crash in five weeks followed by a near-vertical recovery makes almost any two risk assets look like their spread "mean-reverts," because one enormous shared shock dominates that window's variance and then round-trips — not because of a genuine long-run equilibrium. This is a one-off shared-shock artifact, not a standing relationship, and the MCPT's global "best p-value anywhere" test has no way to catch that on its own, since it only checks whether the single best result across the whole screen beats permutation, not whether that result is stable across time.

**The next three FDR q=0.10 survivors tell a softer version of the same story:** HYG/QQQ (6/34 windows), EEM/VNQ (6/39 windows), SPY/VNQ (4/39 windows) all clear p<0.05 more often than nominal 5% would predict, but their hits cluster around known systemic-stress episodes (2008-09 GFC, 2011, 2020 COVID, 2021-23 rate-hike/inflation stress) rather than being spread evenly across calm periods — the same "these assets crash and recover together" signature, just less extreme.

**Design correction (not just a rejected candidate):** picking "the single best pair across the full 20-year sample" is itself a data-mined framing — the pairs-trading analogue of the momentum phase's SHY problem (surviving a correction in isolation is not the same as being economically real). The methodologically standard fix, and the one Gatev-Goetzmann-Rouwenhorst (already a Phase 2 reference) actually specifies, is **walk-forward**: in each formation window, independently, trade whichever pair(s) clear significance using only that window's own data, then re-screen for the next window — never a permanent commitment to one pair. Under that design, the COVID-window's HYG/SPY selection only ever gets traded in one out-of-sample stretch (Feb-Aug 2021), diluted into a portfolio and an aggregate return series that the standard Phase 6a/6b (MCPT/DSR) battery then judges for genuine, non-fluke significance — the same standard momentum's returns were held to, rather than a subjective read of one pair's p-value. See "Phase 3a (Pairs Rule)" below for the implementation.

## Phase 3a (Pairs Rule) — Walk-Forward Pairs Spread-Trading Backtest

**Purpose:** implement the walk-forward correction described above as an actual, validated, runnable spread-trading rule and descriptive backtest.

**Design (decided with the user, 2026-09-24):**
- **Point-in-time pair selection, per formation window:** each of the 63 formation windows independently applies Benjamini-Hochberg FDR at q=0.10 to *only that window's own* tested pairs (`select_pairs_for_window`) — never a pooled, full-sample correction, which would leak future windows' p-values into an earlier window's trading decision.
- **Portfolio, not a single pick:** whatever pairs clear that window's correction (0, 1, or several) are all traded, equally weighted, in the following out-of-sample trading window — the standard Gatev-Goetzmann-Rouwenhorst design (a basket per period), chosen by the user over a single-best-pair-per-window or fixed-top-K alternative specifically because it naturally dilutes a single-window fluke like the COVID-window HYG/SPY selection rather than concentrating risk on it.
- **Divergence/convergence spread rule:** for each selected pair, the spread (`dependent - hedge_ratio*independent - intercept`, using the formation window's own fixed hedge_ratio/intercept, never re-estimated) is z-scored against that SAME formation window's own mean/std. Enter long the spread when z < -2.0, short when z > +2.0, exit when |z| < 0.5, forced-close at the trading window's end if still open. A signal decided as of date t executes on t+1, consistent with this project's standing execution-lag convention.
- **Aggregation:** the final strategy return series is reindexed over the full daily calendar spanning all trading windows, with 0.0 filled in on cash days/windows (no zero-pair window silently creates a "gap" in the series) — a continuous series fit for direct use in Phase 6a/6b-style significance testing.

**Validation (synthetic data, 17/17 checks passing):** the within-window FDR selection exactly matches an independent `multipletests(..., method='fdr_bh')` call and is blind to any other window's rows; the 1-day execution lag is verified directly (no return on the crossing date itself, a real return only from t+1); a forced-close case (spread that never reverts) is confirmed to hold the position through exactly the trading window's last date with no carry-over; a point-in-time check confirms perturbing data strictly after a pair's own trading window leaves its backtested returns completely unchanged; a power check (an engineered OU/mean-reverting spread with two deliberately injected, guaranteed excursions) yields a materially positive average return and multiple realized trades, while an independent-random-walk pair shows no such edge; and a 3-window synthetic aggregation check confirms zero-pair windows contribute exactly 0.0, a two-pair window's portfolio return exactly matches the arithmetic mean of both pairs' own returns, and the final series is gap-free over the full calendar.

**Real-data result:** 50 formation windows (of 63 total; 13 early windows had zero testable pairs due to staggered inception and don't appear), **17 windows with ≥1 selected pair**, averaging 0.84 pairs/window across all 50. The aggregated daily portfolio series has 6,295 observations, 74.5% flat/zero-return days (cash on the sidelines when no pair is selected), **annualized Sharpe = 0.207**, **cumulative return = 34.01%** (descriptive only — not yet significance-tested).

**Window-level concentration diagnostic (requested after the descriptive result, before trusting it):** a window-by-window P&L decomposition of all 17 active windows found the result is **not evenly earned**. Selected highlights (full table in `pairs_walkforward_window_summary.csv`):

| Trading window | # pairs selected | Selected pairs | Window cumulative return |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2002-02-06 | 1 | (QQQ, SPY) | **+20.4%** |
| 2009-02-09 | 3 | (TLT, IWM), (SHY, DBC), (TLT, SPY) | +12.0% |
| 2018-08-15 | 1 | (HYG, GLD) | +9.2% |
| 2021-02-16 | **16** | HYG/SPY, HYG/QQQ, VNQ/EEM, VNQ/SPY, SPY/QQQ, SHY/LQD, VNQ/QQQ, VNQ/HYG, HYG/EEM, VNQ/DBC, VNQ/EFA, HYG/EFA, VNQ/IWM, VNQ/SLV, EFA/EEM, LQD/GLD | -3.5% |

**The single 2002 window (QQQ/SPY, formation spanning the dot-com bust/9-11 period) contributes 60.1% of the strategy's ENTIRE cumulative return.** Separately, the trading window immediately after the COVID formation window (2021-02-16) selected an anomalous **16 pairs simultaneously** — 4-16x more than every other window's 1-4 — and lost money. Both are the same mechanism already diagnosed at the screening stage (Phase 3a Pre-Check): a single dominant, shared shock in a 12-month formation window (the dot-com bust/9-11 for 2002; COVID crash/recovery for 2021) can make many unrelated pairs look cointegrated *simultaneously*, and because their p-values are then highly correlated rather than independent, Benjamini-Hochberg's FDR-control assumptions are strained — letting a whole cluster of shared-shock-driven false discoveries survive together, or (in the QQQ/SPY case) letting one crisis-window pair happen to catch a large, real-looking directional move that has nothing to do with a standing equilibrium relationship.

**Follow-up diagnostic (requested, then explicitly flagged as inconclusive on its own):** excluding the 2002 and 2021 windows, the remaining 15 windows show an even cleaner **cumulative return of 15.75%, annualized Sharpe of 0.246** — encouraging on its face. **But deciding to exclude specific windows *after* seeing which one won and which one lost is itself a form of data snooping** — the same sin as hand-picking "the single best pair" from Phase 2/2b, just run in reverse (removing the inconvenient results instead of cherry-picking the convenient one). It cannot be the basis for a conclusion by itself: either the exclusion rule needs to be specified and justified *ex ante* as its own methodological decision, or the complete, as-built, pre-committed strategy (all 17 windows, no exclusions) needs to be tested for significance as it actually stands. The project took the second path — see "Phase 6a (Pairs Rule)" below.

## Phase 6a (Pairs Rule) — Full-Pipeline Monte Carlo Permutation Test for the Walk-Forward Strategy

**Purpose:** settle the question the window-concentration diagnostic raised, the honest way — test the COMPLETE, AS-BUILT walk-forward strategy (all 17 windows, no exclusions, exactly the pre-committed rule) against a genuine chance-alone null, rather than either trusting the raw 0.207 Sharpe at face value or excluding the two crisis windows post hoc (itself data snooping, per above).

**Design (`step3b_pairtrade_walkforward_mcpt.py`, mirrors `step2b`'s already-agreed rules, extended one level up the pipeline):**
- **Same permutation scheme as Phase 2b, reused as-is (not reimplemented):** each asset's own daily log-returns independently permuted (`permute_panel_per_asset`, imported directly from `step2b_pairtrade_cointegration_mcpt.py`), destroying every real pairwise relationship while preserving each series' own marginal distribution, random-walk-ness, and staggered inception.
- **Trial 0 = real data; trials 1..n_trials = permuted.** Critically, **every trial re-runs the ENTIRE pipeline end-to-end**: screen (`run_rolling_cointegration_screen`, fixed ADF lag=1, same tractability reason as Phase 2b) → within-window FDR selection → spread backtest (`run_walkforward_pairs_backtest`, same q=0.10/entry_z=2.0/exit_z=0.5 as the real Phase 3a run) — not a pre-computed screen reused across trials, since a permuted panel needs its own fresh screen and could select an entirely different set of pairs in an entirely different pattern of windows.
- **Two statistics, each its own one-sided Monte Carlo p-value:** `sharpe` (annualized, of the trial's aggregate daily portfolio-return series) and `cum_return` (total compounded return over the trial's full traded calendar) — is the observed value more extreme than a chance-alone search-and-select procedure of this exact kind would produce?
- **Same checkpoint/resume + `ProcessPoolExecutor` parallelization pattern as Phase 2b's MCPT**, for the same Colab-disconnect reasons already learned the hard way there.

**Validation (synthetic data, 15/15 checks passing):** trial bookkeeping (n_trials+1 rows, trial 0 correctly flagged real); the MC p-value formulas for both `sharpe` and `cum_return` verified against an independent recomputation; a determinism check confirming the internal per-trial worker, called standalone on the real panel, exactly reproduces trial 0's numbers inside a full run; a **power check** (one pair genuinely and persistently cointegrated across every formation window, embedded among unrelated assets) whose real Sharpe/cumulative return clearly stand out from the permutation null (most permuted trials never find a significant, FDR-surviving pair at all, since permutation destroys the cross-asset relationship); a **null check** reusing the exact panel recipe already proven in Phase 2b not to fool the screening-level MCPT (assets correlated via a shared daily return factor — the same "crisis-shared-shock" shape this whole module exists to guard against — but each keeping its own independent stochastic trend, i.e. genuinely not cointegrated) correctly does NOT produce a standout result; `n_jobs=1` vs. `n_jobs>1` give identical results at the full-pipeline level (using the same `importlib.util` real-module-loading workaround as Phase 2b's test, since `ProcessPoolExecutor` needs a genuinely importable module to pickle the worker function against); and a checkpoint/resume check confirms an interrupted-and-resumed run matches a clean run exactly, one level up the pipeline from Phase 2b's equivalent check.

**Runtime note:** each trial still re-runs the full ~7,200-regression screen (the dominant cost, ~21s previously benchmarked in Phase 2b) plus a comparatively cheap backtest on top — so the cost profile is essentially the same as Phase 2b's own MCPT run. At the user's chosen **n_trials=150** (picked over ~75 or ~250, given the known Colab compute constraints from Phase 2b), that's roughly 150 × ~21s ≈ 52 minutes serial, faster parallelized across cores — with the same checkpoint/resume support, so a Colab disconnect costs minutes, not the whole run.

**Test-harness fix (found after the user re-ran the test file locally):** the checkpoint/resume check compared the two runs' summary dicts with exact `==`, which is too strict — after a resume, the summary's `permutation_sharpe_mean/std` and `permutation_cum_return_mean/std` are computed from a mix of freshly-computed (full-precision, in-memory) and checkpoint-recovered (CSV-round-tripped) trial rows, and a CSV round-trip can differ from the in-memory float by a bit or two. This is the exact same expected, harmless artifact already documented (and already handled with `np.allclose`) for the full per-trial-table check immediately below it in the same file — the summary-level check had inconsistently kept the stricter exact-equality comparison inherited from `step2b`'s test template. Fixed to use the same tolerant (`np.isclose`) comparison; re-run, all 15/15 checks pass. Not a bug in `step3b_pairtrade_walkforward_mcpt.py` itself — the module's own logic was never in question, only the test's comparison strictness.

**Real-data result (150 trials, ~10s via checkpoint resume from a prior partial run):**

| Statistic | Observed (real data, trial 0) | Permutation null | MC p-value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sharpe (annualized) | 0.047 | mean -0.008, std 0.206 | **0.3775 — not significant** |
| Cumulative return | 2.30% | mean -3.34%, std 32.66% | **0.3775 — not significant** |

**Note on trial 0 vs. the original Phase 3a descriptive numbers (0.207 Sharpe / 34.0% cumulative return):** these do NOT match, and that's expected, not a bug — the same situation already flagged in Phase 2b's own docstring. `step3b`'s screen step uses a **fixed ADF lag=1** (needed for tractability across 150 trials), while the original Phase 3a run's screen (`pairs_cointegration_screen_all.csv`) used the **AIC-selected lag**. Different lag orders shift the Engle-Granger p-values, which changes which pairs clear each window's FDR filter, which changes which trades actually happen — so a different (fixed-lag) real Sharpe/cumulative return is expected. What matters for the significance verdict is trial 0 vs. its OWN permutation null under identical (fixed-lag) settings, which is exactly what this table reports.

**Verdict — null, and a clean one:** the observed Sharpe (0.047) is only ~0.27 permutation-standard-deviations above the permutation mean; the observed cumulative return (2.30%) is only ~0.17 permutation-standard-deviations above its mean. Neither is remotely in the tail of its own chance-alone null — MC p=0.3775 means roughly 38% of permutation trials did at least as well. **This is the honest answer the window-concentration diagnostic was pointing toward:** once the complete, as-built, pre-committed walk-forward strategy (screen → within-window FDR select → backtest, all real windows, no post-hoc exclusions) is judged against a genuine null of that exact search-and-select procedure, its performance is indistinguishable from chance. Combined with the screening-level artifact (Phase 3a Pre-Check) and the portfolio-level concentration diagnosis (above), this is now a decisive, three-times-independently-corroborated conclusion: **statistical-arbitrage pairs trading on this 14-asset universe, with this formation/trading window design, does not have real, non-fluke edge that this project's testing can detect** — the pairs-trading analogue of time-series momentum's own null conclusion.

## Strategic Decision — Falling Back to a Single-Asset Rule (Bollinger Bands)

With pairs trading concluded null (Phase 6a above), the user was asked how to proceed: try a different pairs-trading design, declare the primary-rule search concluded with two documented nulls, or fall back to a simpler single-asset technical rule. **Decided: fall back to a single-asset rule** — specifically **Bollinger Bands**, chosen over RSI and over a combined pre-committed grid of both, when asked directly.

**Why Bollinger Bands and not RSI or MACD:** MACD was already ruled out earlier as too close to time-series momentum's own (already-null) trend-following hypothesis family — it risks the same null result for the same underlying reason. Between Bollinger Bands and RSI (both mean-reversion, a genuinely different hypothesis from momentum), Bollinger Bands was chosen specifically because its z-score/entry-exit logic is the same shape as the spread-trading rule already built and validated for pairs trading (step3a's `backtest_one_pair_window` state machine) — much of that machinery (the entry/exit state machine, the 1-day execution-lag convention) carries over directly, just applied to a single asset's own price instead of a pair's spread.

## Phase 2 (Bollinger Bands Rule) — Signal Construction

**Purpose:** the Bollinger rule's equivalent of Phase 2 — per-asset rolling z-score and a long/short/flat position state machine, independently for each of the 14 assets (`bb_step2_signal_construction.py`).

**Design (methodology choices flagged explicitly, per standing practice):**
- **Mean-reversion (contrarian), not breakout.** Bollinger Bands can be traded either way; this project deliberately uses the mean-reversion interpretation (trade AGAINST a band crossing), the same reason pairs trading was chosen over momentum-family alternatives — it's a genuinely different hypothesis from trend-following.
- **Rolling window = 20 trading days**, Bollinger's own standard textbook default, not tuned on this data — the same discipline already applied to pairs trading's entry_z/exit_z and momentum's N=3/6/12 grid.
- **entry_z=2.0 / exit_z=0.5**, reused as-is from the already-validated pairs-trading spread rule, rather than a fresh, untested threshold pair (and Bollinger's own standard convention of 2 standard deviations independently agrees with entry_z=2.0).
- **Log-price**, not raw price, for the rolling mean/std — consistent with every other module in this project, and more appropriate given how far some assets (SPY in particular) have grown over their history.
- **Per-asset, fully independent** — no cross-asset information used anywhere (unlike pairs trading). `pandas.rolling(window, min_periods=window)` only starts counting once an asset's own real observations begin, so staggered inception is handled correctly for free, consistent with Phase 0b's per-asset-independent principle.
- **Continuous, not window-bounded** — unlike pairs trading's 12mo-formation/6mo-trading design, there is no periodic re-estimation window; the rolling z-score recomputes daily from that day's own trailing 20 observations, and a position simply runs forward (long/short/flat) with no forced-close boundary, exactly like momentum's monthly-updated position.
- **1-day execution lag**, this project's standing convention.

**Validation (synthetic data, 15/15 checks passing):** the z-score computation matches an independently-computed reference (a plain Python loop doing explicit windowed slicing, not reusing pandas' own `.rolling()`) element-for-element, including exact NaN-position agreement; staggered inception produces the first valid z-score at exactly the expected row (never earlier or later, ruling out both look-ahead and an off-by-one); per-asset independence and point-in-time-ness are each confirmed by direct perturbation checks; the long/short/flat state machine is checked directly against hand-picked z-arrays covering entry, exit, a position that holds all the way to the end of the series with no forced-close, boundary values exactly AT the entry/exit thresholds (confirming strict, not weak, inequality), and NaN always forcing flat even mid-trade; and a mechanism check confirms an engineered mean-reverting series triggers both a long and a short round-trip while a pure, zero-noise linear trend (a rolling mean's well-understood constant lag behind a steady trend, still safely under the entry threshold) never spuriously enters a position. Two test-authoring mistakes were caught and fixed while building this suite (not module bugs): a hand-computed "expected" state sequence that didn't match the state machine's own (correct) trace once worked through by hand, and a "smooth trend" scenario whose added noise was inadvertently large relative to the tiny trend's own local rolling std, making occasional spurious entries the statistically expected outcome rather than a bug — both fixed by correcting the test's own expectations/inputs, not the module.

**Real-data result:** run on the actual 14-asset panel. Per-asset valid-day/long-day/short-day counts came back well-formed for every asset (e.g. SPY: 8,269 valid days, 1,243 long-days, 2,010 short-days; QQQ: 6,727/1,118/1,632; DBC: 4,990/959/1,093), with each asset's own staggered inception correctly reflected in its valid-day count.

**Output artifacts:** `data/raw/bollinger_zscore_14assets.csv`, `data/raw/bollinger_daily_position_14assets.csv`.

## Phase 3a (Bollinger Bands Rule) — Real-Data Result & Conclusion

**Purpose:** combine Phase 2's positions with realized returns across all 14 assets, each sliced to its own tradeable window, for a descriptive Bollinger-vs-buy-and-hold comparison — the direct analogue of the original momentum Phase 3a (`bb_step3a_backtest.py`).

There is no such combination here — a single asset's own directional bet — so this module uses actual simple returns from the TRI level (`price_panel[assets].pct_change(fill_method=None)`, the `fill_method=None` a forward-compatibility fix for a pandas `FutureWarning` about the deprecated `fill_method='pad'` default, no logic change), the more standard, realistic choice, consistent with how the original momentum Phase 3a treated "realized returns." The z-score signal itself is still computed on log price.

**Validation (synthetic data, 11/11 checks passing):** the per-asset tradeable window (from first valid z-score date onward) is confirmed exactly against an independent computation; the 1-day execution lag is checked directly (an entry day itself earns zero return, the following day correctly earns `position × that day's simple return`); the strategy-return formula is cross-checked against an independent recomputation straight from the raw price panel; the buy-and-hold comparison's Sharpe/cumulative-return are independently recomputed and matched exactly; cross-asset independence is confirmed by perturbing one asset and checking every other asset's output is untouched; and an asset too short to ever accumulate a single valid rolling window is confirmed to produce a well-formed all-NaN row/column rather than crashing the aggregation loop. ## Strategic Decision — Declaring Bollinger Bands Concluded Null

**The fork:** given how decisively negative the descriptive result above is, the user was asked how to proceed — run the formal MCPT/DSR significance battery anyway for a complete, consistent record (the same standard momentum and pairs trading were both held to); skip formal testing and declare the rule concluded null now; or build one explicitly pre-specified, clearly-labeled design variant (e.g. a trend filter disabling counter-trend shorts) as a genuinely new hypothesis, not a retune of the failed design.

**Decided: skip formal significance testing and declare Bollinger Bands concluded null now.** Reasoning: a permutation test asks whether the *observed* statistic is more extreme than what the same search-and-select procedure produces on randomly shuffled data. With 7 of 14 assets showing outright negative raw Sharpe and 0 of 14 beating buy-and-hold, the observed effect is not just weak — it is negative-to-near-zero across the board. A negative-to-near-zero raw Sharpe cannot come back with a small (significant) one-sided MC p-value against a mean-zero-ish chance null; running the MCPT/DSR battery here would be confirming an outcome that is already mathematically foreclosed by the descriptive result, not testing a genuinely open question. This is a departure from how momentum and pairs trading were each still put through formal testing despite weak-looking descriptive results — the difference is that those descriptive results were weak-but-mixed-sign (some assets/pairs positive, ambiguous whether it was signal or noise), while Bollinger Bands' result is coherently, diagnosably negative in a specific direction (the short leg fighting a secular uptrend) — a qualitatively different, stronger basis for skipping the formal step without eyeballing past real uncertainty.

**Consequence:** the OPEN methodology question about whether `step6_mcpt_permutation_test.py`'s internal N-grid-search design generalizes to a non-searched single rule (flagged when Bollinger Bands was first designed) is now moot for this rule — step6 was never run against it. **The question was later answered anyway, for a different rule** — see "Phase 6 (Seasonality Rule)" below, which needed and built its own dedicated (and much simpler) MCPT module rather than reusing step6. **A trend-aware, risk-managed variant of this same mean-reversion hypothesis was eventually built and tested as this project's sixth primary rule — see "Phase 12" below.**

**This is now the third consecutive null primary rule** on this 14-asset universe (time-series momentum, statistical arbitrage / pairs trading, Bollinger Bands mean-reversion), each concluded null via a distinct route: momentum via a formally-tested near-zero unconditional/conditional edge; pairs trading via a formally-tested chance-level edge that traced to crisis-window artifacts at two separate pipeline stages; Bollinger Bands via an outright negative descriptive effect traced to a specific, diagnosable mechanism (shorting into secular uptrends), not requiring formal testing to rule out.

**Whether to search for a fourth primary rule or move to Phase 9 (audit & writeup of all three null findings) was raised with the user and explicitly deferred — the user chose to pause and decide later rather than commit now.**

## Strategic Decision — Choosing a Fourth Primary Rule (Seasonality)

**Context:** in a later session, the user opened a new notebook, describing the existing one as "too much" (trend-following + pairs trading + Bollinger Bands all in one place), and — visibly fatigued after three nulls — brought a reference book (Kaufman, *Trading Systems and Methods*, 5th ed., user-uploaded as `TSaM.pdf`) rather than a specific new-rule proposal, asking explicitly to have it read rather than reading it themselves.

**Reading approach:** rather than reading the whole ~900-page book, five chapters were targeted specifically for candidates that do NOT share momentum's, pairs trading's, or Bollinger Bands' common ancestor (all three derive their trading decision from an asset's own, or another asset's, price history): Ch. 10 (Seasonality/calendar effects), Ch. 12 (Volume/Open Interest/Breadth), Ch. 13 (Carry Trade), Ch. 14 (Commitment of Traders Report), Ch. 19 (Multiple Time Frames).

**Candidates surfaced and their honest assessment, presented to the user before any code was written:**
- **Seasonality (calendar effects)** — genuinely different mechanism (trades on the date, not the price path), zero new data required, but multiple documented variants exist in the chapter (month-end effect, holiday effect, Hirsch/"Sell in May", January Barometer) — picking one only after seeing which "worked" would just be slower data mining, so ONE effect needed to be pre-committed before any real-data run.
- **Volume-based indicators (single-asset: OBV, Force Index, PVI/NVI)** — a new information source (flow, not just price), but still squarely in the same broad "single-asset technical analysis" family as Bollinger Bands; assessed as only moderately different.
- **Cross-sectional breadth as a countertrend signal** — the book's own documented finding is a genuine mean-reversion signal, but breadth is a market-wide construct built for hundreds of securities; reinterpreting it across only 14 ETFs was assessed as too statistically thin to be a credible test, not a live candidate.
- **Carry trade** and **the COT report** — both genuinely different (fundamentals/positioning-driven, not price-pattern-driven), but both require data this project's WRDS pipeline does not have (FX/interest-rate differentials; CFTC futures positioning) — flagged as good ideas requiring a new data-sourcing project, not something buildable today.
- **Multiple time frames** — explicitly NOT recommended despite needing no new data: stripped of its multi-horizon packaging, the underlying bet (price extrapolates) is momentum's own already-null hypothesis, just re-sliced by horizon — building it risked re-litigating a settled question under a new name.

## Phase 2 (Seasonality Rule) — Signal Construction

**Purpose:** the seasonality rule's equivalent of Phase 2 — a shared, price-independent calendar signal, applied identically to every asset except for per-asset inception masking (`season_step2_signal_construction.py`).

**Design (methodology choices pre-committed and flagged explicitly, exactly as for every prior rule):**
- **One specific, pre-registered effect: the month-end effect**, not a scan across the half-dozen calendar patterns Kaufman documents. Picked over Sell-in-May (~2 trades/year/asset) and the January Barometer (~1 binary bet/year) specifically for **statistical power** — month-end fires ~12x/year/asset, giving on the order of 200+ independent monthly cycles across this project's sample, versus ~20-40 for the other two, which cannot support a meaningful permutation test. Also favored for **documented cross-asset generality** (confirmed across both equity indices and bonds in Kaufman's own text), matching this project's mixed universe better than an effect documented mainly for one equity index.
- **Exact window: long the last 2 trading days of the month through the first 4 trading days of the following month** (Kaufman's own stated variant), computed purely from trading-day rank within each `(year, month)` group on the shared panel calendar — no fitted parameter, no lookback estimation window at all (unlike every prior rule).
- **Long-only, not long/short.** The month-end effect as documented is a one-sided claim (returns are anomalously high in this window), not a symmetric contrarian bet — manufacturing an invented short leg outside the window would test a different, unstated hypothesis, not Kaufman's.
- **Calendar rank computed off the shared panel trading calendar**, not per-asset, mirroring how Bollinger's rolling window used the shared panel index as its time backbone; a not-yet-alive asset simply gets a flat (0) signal until its own first valid price.
- **1-day execution lag, kept even though this rule doesn't strictly need it.** Every prior rule's lag exists because its signal is decided FROM that day's own market data and can only be traded the next day; a calendar signal is known arbitrarily far in advance. The lag was applied anyway, uniformly, so this rule isn't quietly given a more favorable execution assumption than the other three got.

**Validation (synthetic data, 12/12 checks passing):** the flagged dates for a hand-built calendar with simulated holidays (irregular trading-day counts per month, so a calendar-day shortcut couldn't accidentally pass the check) exactly match an independently hand-derived expected set; month-boundary adjacency and middle-of-month exclusion are each checked directly; staggered inception is confirmed to zero out the signal before an asset's own first valid price and pick it up correctly from that date onward; cross-asset independence and price-magnitude invariance (the signal depends only on which dates are non-NaN, never on price levels) are both confirmed by direct perturbation.

**Output artifacts:** `data/raw/season_daily_position_14assets.csv`.

## Phase 3a (Seasonality Rule) — Real-Data Descriptive Result

**Purpose:** combine Phase 2's calendar signal with realized simple returns, the direct analogue of every prior rule's own Phase 3a (`season_step3a_backtest.py`).

**A genuinely new edge case, caught while building the test suite (not a data problem):** this rule's tradeable window can begin on an asset's literal first price observation, where `pct_change()` is mathematically undefined (no prior price to diff against) — `0 (flat, pre-position) × NaN (undefined return) = NaN`, not the intended `0.0`. Every prior rule's own lookback window happened to guarantee a valid prior price already existed by the time its signal went live, so this exact case never came up before. Fixed by slicing each asset's return series from the first date a return is actually DEFINED (via `simple_rets[asset].dropna()`), not the first date it has a price — the same one-day distinction now documented directly in the module's own docstring so it isn't lost.

**A second bug, caught via cross-checking against this module's own backtest:** `_ann_sharpe`'s `ret.std()` call defaults to `ddof=1` (sample std) for a pandas Series but `ddof=0` (population std) for a plain numpy array — so calling the identical function from a pandas-based caller (this module) and a numpy-array-based caller (the permutation-test module, below) silently produced two different Sharpe ratios for the "same" real result. Fixed by passing `ddof=1` explicitly, matching this project's own established convention (momentum's `step6` module already does this explicitly, for the same reason).

**Interpretive caveat, built into the module's own output rather than left implicit:** this rule is in the market only ~28.6% of trading days by construction (6 of a typical ~21-day month). A naive "beats buy-and-hold on Sharpe" comparison is a materially weaker read here than it was for the other three (largely fully-invested) rules — under a pure chance null with no real calendar effect, mixing ~71% exactly-zero-return days into the Sharpe calculation is expected to shrink the strategy's own annualized Sharpe to roughly half of buy-and-hold's, for the same underlying return distribution (a "flat-day-dilution" effect, not evidence of anything real by itself). The `comparison` table therefore also reports `pct_days_exposed` explicitly.

**Real-data result:** the strategy beat buy-and-hold on raw Sharpe in only **1 of 14 assets** (EEM, and only marginally: 0.4702 vs. 0.4665) — but read against the ~0.5x-dilution expectation above rather than a flat 1.0x comparison, the picture is genuinely mixed, not decisively negative: 8 of 14 assets (EEM, HYG, QQQ, SPY, SLV, GLD, LQD, SHY) show a strategy-to-buy-and-hold Sharpe ratio ABOVE the ~0.535 dilution-only expectation, while 6 (DBC, EFA, IEF, IWM, TLT, VNQ) fall below it — roughly a coin flip across the universe, unlike Bollinger Bands' decisively one-sided result. **This is exactly the kind of ambiguous descriptive result that, per this project's own established practice (momentum and pairs trading were both formally tested on similarly weak-but-mixed descriptive results), calls for a real significance test rather than a judgment call either way** — see "Phase 6 (Seasonality Rule)" below.

**Output artifacts:** `data/raw/season_strategy_returns_14assets.csv`, `data/raw/season_vs_buyhold_comparison_14assets.csv`.

## Phase 6 (Seasonality Rule) — Monte Carlo Permutation Test & Conclusion

**Why this needed its own module:** an earlier assumption in this log (recorded when Bollinger Bands was designed) held that `step6_mcpt_permutation_test.py`/`step6b_deflated_sharpe_ratio.py` would be "reusable as-is for any new primary rule." In practice, across all three rules tried after momentum (pairs trading, Bollinger Bands, and now seasonality), that has never once been true for step6 — its actual implementation is hard-wired to momentum's own N=3/6/12 grid-search mechanics. Every rule so far has needed its own dedicated significance-testing module sharing the same DESIGN PRINCIPLES (permute returns, keep dates fixed, rerun the rule's own mechanics per trial, compare trial 0 to the permutation distribution), never the same literal code. This is now recorded as the corrected, general lesson (see "Open Methodology Watch-Items" below), replacing the earlier one-off "moot for Bollinger Bands" framing.

**Good news specific to this rule:** seasonality has no "search and pick best" step at all — `n_end=2`/`n_start=4` was pre-committed from Kaufman's text, never tuned on this data — so there's no training-bias decomposition to build (unlike momentum's step6). Better still, because the calendar mask never depends on price data, it is IDENTICAL across every permuted trial — no per-trial re-optimization of any kind, only reapplying one fixed mask to a reshuffled return array. This made the full test cheap enough to run at NREPS=2000 with no checkpointing infrastructure: **2000 trials × 14 assets completed in 4.8 seconds**, a sharp contrast to pairs trading's ~52-minute, checkpoint-and-resume-requiring runs.

**Design (`season_step6_mcpt.py`):** per-asset (14 independent tests, same reasoning as momentum's step6 — pooling first would hide which assets, if any, show something real); trial 0 = real data, trials 1..1999 permute each asset's own simple daily returns (shuffle values, keep the fixed calendar mask and dates in place); two one-sided statistics (annualized Sharpe, cumulative return), each its own Monte Carlo p-value.

**Validation (synthetic data, 15/15 checks passing):** trial-0 consistency — the module's own "real" statistic exactly matches `season_step3a_backtest.py`'s independently-computed one (this check is what caught the `ddof` bug above); permutation is confirmed to be a genuine reordering of the same value multiset, never a resample; p-value bounds and run-to-run determinism are both checked directly; a **power check** (an engineered asset with a real, additive excess return baked in only on calendar-flagged days) returns a small p-value on both statistics; a **calibration check** across 8 independently-seeded no-effect synthetic assets shows p-values are not systematically small (0/8 below 0.05); cross-asset independence is confirmed by perturbation.

## Phase 10 — Single-Asset Carry Trade (TLT)

**Design (`carry_signal.py`, pre-committed before touching real data):** bond carry ≈ yield minus the risk-free rate (Koijen, Moskowitz, Pedersen & Vrugt, 2018, "Carry"). For TLT (20+ year duration), the natural proxy is the term spread **DGS20 (20-year Treasury Constant Maturity) minus DGS3MO (3-month T-bill)**, both from FRED. Signal = sign of the spread (long when the curve is normal/upward-sloping, short when inverted), computed on the intersection of valid dates for both series (no forward-filling across a data gap), ties broken long. `_ann_sharpe` uses `ddof=1` explicitly, this project's now-standing convention for any shared stats helper.

**Data-sourcing note:** the sandbox cannot reach `fred.stlouisfed.org` directly (confirmed via a failed `WebFetch` and a failed direct `curl`, 403 from the outbound proxy) — the user downloaded the public, credential-free FRED CSV directly and attached it. **Verified before trusting it**, per this project's hard-learned data-integrity lesson (see "Data Integrity Incident" above): confirmed the well-documented DGS20 historical discontinuation gap (no data January 1987–September 1993, when the 20-year bond wasn't issued) appears correctly in the file.

**Validation (synthetic data, `test_carry_signal.py`, 8/8 checks passing):** sign correctness (positive/negative/zero-tie spread), spread-value match, gap handling (no forward-fill across missing dates), scale invariance, determinism.

**Bug caught and fixed (`carry_backtest.py`):** the initial implementation restricted the signal to its intersection with TLT's own return dates *before* applying the 1-day execution lag, which incorrectly zeroed out the very first day of TLT's tradeable window — even though genuine prior-day yield-curve data exists (FRED's series predate TLT's 2002 inception by decades). Caught by `test_carry_backtest.py`'s independent-recomputation check disagreeing with the buggy version at the boundary. **Fixed by shifting the full, unrestricted signal first, then intersecting with TLT's own dates** — a general "restrict-then-shift" bug pattern now flagged for any future lagged-signal design (see "Open Methodology Watch-Items"). Re-validated: 10/10 checks passing after the fix, including two new checks specifically covering the corrected boundary behavior.

**MCPT design (`carry_mcpt.py`):** no in-sample optimization step needed (the signal has no fitted parameter), so — unlike momentum's `step6` — this is a single, direct permutation test, the cheapest shape in this project's whole MCPT lineup alongside seasonality's. TLT's own daily returns are permuted (shuffled values, keep dates fixed); the executed carry signal is identical across every trial, since it never depends on TLT's price. Trial 0 = real data, NREPS=2000, seed=20260924, matching project convention. Validated on synthetic data (`test_carry_mcpt.py`, 8/8 checks passing): trial-0 consistency, permutation-is-a-reordering, p-value bounds, determinism, a power check (engineered signal-correlated effect), and a null/calibration check (8 independent draws).

**Real-data result:**

| Statistic | Strategy | Buy-and-hold |
|---|---|---|
| Annualized Sharpe | 0.3496 | 0.3284 |
| Cumulative return | 153.0% | 135.7% |

n_obs = 5,851 trading days, 88.1% of days signaled long (consistent with the yield curve spending most of the sample in a normal, upward-sloping shape).

**Monte Carlo Permutation Test:** p_value_sharpe = 0.229, p_value_cum_return = 0.229 — **not significant.** The strategy's modest edge over buy-and-hold is not distinguishable from what a randomly-reordered version of TLT's own return history would produce roughly 23% of the time.

**Interpretation:** this is a fifth null result, but via a genuinely different mechanism than any of the first four — not a weak or absent effect, but a plausible, directionally-correct one (mechanically, holding long-duration bonds when the curve is normal and cutting exposure when it inverts is a reasonable, textbook-grounded bet) that simply doesn't clear statistical significance, most likely because the underlying phenomenon (yield-curve inversions) is a **regime-level event that occurs only a handful of times across the sample** — the same few-independent-events power problem already seen with Sell-in-May and the January Barometer during the seasonality candidate survey, now recurring for a genuinely different rule family.

## Phase 10b — Duration Extension (TLT/IEF/SHY) and the Case Against Naive Pooling

**Context:** after reporting the TLT carry result, the user asked to "show me" — interpreted as wanting both a visual chart and an extension of the analysis, given the natural next question (does pooling across more Treasury-duration assets sharpen the signal?). The same carry signal and mechanics (`carry_signal.py`/`carry_backtest.py`/`carry_mcpt.py`, unchanged) were applied to IEF (7-10yr) and SHY (1-3yr) in addition to TLT (20+yr) — all three already present in the main project's 14-asset universe.

**Real-data result (all three assets, same DGS20-DGS3MO signal):**

| Asset (duration) | Strat Ann. Sharpe | B&H Ann. Sharpe | MC p-value |
|---|---|---|---|
| TLT (20+ yr) | 0.350 | 0.328 | 0.229 |
| IEF (7-10 yr) | 0.489 | 0.532 | 0.263 |
| SHY (1-3 yr) | 0.717 | 1.285 | 0.974 |

**Finding (unplanned, surfaced by actually running the extension rather than assuming it would work):** the carry signal doesn't just weaken for shorter durations — the strategy actively **hurts** SHY, cutting its Sharpe from 1.285 (buy-and-hold) down to 0.717. **Mechanism:** an inverted yield curve is bad for a long-duration bond (the textbook carry-trade logic TLT's signal was built around), but it's actually **good** for a short-duration instrument like SHY, whose income is driven by short-term rates directly — when short rates rise enough to invert the curve, SHY's own yield rises too. The same term-spread sign-flip that correctly signals "reduce long-duration exposure" is **directionally backwards** when applied uniformly to a short-duration instrument.

**Decision: reject naive pooling.** The original motivation for extending to IEF/SHY was more statistical power via a larger pooled sample — but pooling three assets where the same signal has a genuine, mechanically-explained sign reversal across them would dilute or actively contaminate the result, not strengthen it. This is a genuine discovery about the *design*, not just a null result: any future carry-based test on this Treasury curve needs either duration-specific signal construction (e.g., a signal built from a maturity-matched point on the curve) or an explicit split by duration bucket, not a single spread applied indiscriminately across the yield-curve.

**Output artifact:** `carry_tlt_ief_shy_comparison.png` (4-panel chart: term spread with regime shading, plus each asset's cumulative strategy-vs-buy-and-hold curves).

## Phase 11 — Dow-30 Book-to-Market Value Factor (In Progress)

**Purpose:** this project's first rule built on **fundamental data** rather than market data — a genuinely different AFML data type from all five prior rules (momentum, pairs trading, Bollinger Bands, seasonality, carry), all of which are price/return-derived.

**Design (`value_signal.py`, pre-committed before any real Compustat/CCM data was pulled or examined):**
- **Universe:** the Dow 30, a fixed, externally-defined list (not hand-picked for this test).
- **Signal: book-to-market = CEQ (book common equity) / ME (market equity).** CEQ taken from the most recent fiscal year ending *before* January 1 of the formation year — guaranteeing at least ~6 months' reporting lag, the standard Fama-French convention (June-year-t portfolios use fiscal-year-(t-1) book equity). ME = |price| × shares outstanding, as of the nearest trading day at or before December 31 of the prior year — the same fiscal-year boundary CEQ eligibility uses.
- **Portfolio formation: annual, every June 30.** Rank all 30 stocks by B/M descending; long the top 10 ("value"), short the bottom 10 ("growth"), equal-weighted within each leg, flat on the middle 10. Hold 12 months, then re-rank. No fitted parameter — the 10/10/10 split, June formation, and 12-month hold are all standard convention, not tuned on this data.
- **1-day execution lag**, this project's standing convention, on top of the annual rebalance.

**WRDS pull script (`wrds_value_factor_pull.py`, sent to the user):** three tables instead of the usual two — `comp.funda` (Compustat annual fundamentals, for CEQ), `crsp.ccmxpf_linktable` (the CRSP-Compustat Merged link table, bridging Compustat's GVKEY to CRSP's PERMNO via `linktype IN ('LC','LU')` and `linkprim IN ('P','C')` — the standard, WRDS-documented "primary, current link only" filter), and `crsp.dsf_v2`/`crsp.stksecurityinfohist` (daily prices + shares outstanding, same as every prior pull in this project). ## Strategic Decision — Designing a Sixth Primary Rule With Risk Management Built In

**Context:** with Phase 11 built and awaiting its WRDS pull, the user asked whether Pillar 2 (risk management — volatility targeting, drawdown control) could be retrofitted onto one of the five already-null rules, to make progress on that pillar while the value factor's data was pending.

**Honest assessment given before any code was written:** retrofitting risk management onto an already-null rule is methodologically risky in general — it invites quietly re-litigating a settled null result by dressing it up with stop-losses and sizing until something looks better, the same "search until it works" pattern this project's whole MCPT/DSR discipline exists to guard against. One exception was flagged as worth considering: Bollinger Bands' diagnosed failure mode (a two-sided contrarian rule structurally shorting into each asset's own secular uptrend, concluded in "Strategic Decision — Declaring Bollinger Bands Concluded Null" above) is precisely the kind of mechanism a trend filter could plausibly fix — but even there, retrofitting risk management onto that same rule would blur "testing a new hypothesis" with "patching a rejected one."

**Decided (user's explicit choice, "yes please" — a typo for "yes please," confirmed in context): design an original strategy with risk management built in from the start**, rather than retrofit one onto any of the five null rules. This is the first rule in this project not adapted wholesale from a single named external reference (Kaufman, Koijen et al., Fama-French) — instead it deliberately recombines two of the project's own already-validated pieces into a new hybrid hypothesis: Bollinger Bands' own 20-day z-score mean-reversion trigger (Phase 2, Bollinger rule), gated by a 252-day trend filter in the spirit of momentum's own signal (Phase 2, momentum), so that a mean-reversion trade is only taken in the direction of the asset's own longer-term trend — directly targeting Bollinger Bands' diagnosed failure mode rather than assuming it away.

**Options-hedging tangent (raised, then deferred):** the user also asked whether an options-based hedge overlay — using their confirmed WRDS OptionMetrics access — could be part of the risk-management design here. Deferred to a future "Phase C": building option-pricing/greeks machinery and pulling a wholly new OptionMetrics dataset is a substantial separate undertaking, out of scope for getting a first pass at a risk-managed trend-filtered mean-reversion rule built and tested. Recorded as a live option for future risk-management work, not abandoned.

**Universe:** the same 14-asset ETF universe as every prior rule (explicit user confirmation) — no new WRDS pull needed, unlike the concurrent Phase 11 value-factor thread, which is still waiting on its own refreshed 30-ticker pull.

## Phase 12a — Trend-Filtered Mean-Reversion: Signal Construction

**Design (`trend_meanrev_signal.py`, pre-committed before touching real data):**
- **Mean-reversion trigger:** identical to Bollinger Bands' own mechanics (Phase 2, Bollinger rule) — 20-day rolling log-price z-score, entry_z=2.0, exit_z=0.5 — reused unchanged rather than re-tuned, consistent with this project's standing discipline of not treating a textbook default as a free parameter.
- **Trend filter (the new piece):** a 252-day (roughly one trading year) trailing trend direction computed independently per asset. A mean-reversion entry is only taken when it agrees with the asset's own current 252-day trend — e.g. only buy an oversold dip (z < -2.0) if the 252-day trend is still up; only short an overbought spike (z > +2.0) if the 252-day trend is down. A signal that would fight the prevailing trend is suppressed (flat) rather than taken, directly targeting the mechanism Bollinger Bands' own Phase 3a diagnosed (shorting "overbought" conditions in assets that then kept climbing).
- **Per-asset independence and staggered inception**, consistent with every prior rule's per-asset-independent principle — the 252-day trend filter's own lookback means an asset's tradeable window starts later than its raw 20-day z-score would alone (HYG, the universe's youngest asset at 2007-04-11 inception, doesn't clear the 252-day requirement until ~2008-04).
- **1-day execution lag**, this project's standing convention.

**Validation:** built and validated on synthetic data before this session's combined-backtest work began, confirming the trend-filter gating logic correctly suppresses counter-trend entries while leaving with-trend entries and the exit rule untouched, and that per-asset staggered inception is handled independently (no pooled cutoff).

## Phase 12b — Risk Management Overlay

**Design (`risk_overlay.py`, three independent, composable pieces, pre-committed before touching real data):**
1. **Stop-loss / take-profit**, evaluated against the *unsized* position — so a fixed-volatility-multiple stop triggers at the same underlying price move regardless of what position size is eventually applied, keeping the risk-trigger and the sizing decision independent of each other.
2. **Volatility-based position-size multiplier** — each asset's position is scaled by a cross-sectional-median-volatility-relative multiplier (capped and floored), so a currently-calm asset isn't sized identically to a currently-turbulent one.
3. **Portfolio-level maximum-drawdown circuit breaker** — trigger at a 15% portfolio drawdown, resume once drawdown recovers to a lower threshold; applied once at the PORTFOLIO level (equal-weighted across whichever assets are currently tradeable), never per-asset, and the resulting on/off multiplier is applied uniformly back to every asset's own P&L on that date.

**Bug self-identified and fixed (the circuit breaker's own on/off logic):** the original implementation decided whether to trigger or resume using the *realized* (already breaker-cut) equity curve — but once the breaker cuts a return to 0%, realized equity freezes and can never recover back toward its old peak on its own, permanently trapping the breaker ON after a single trigger. **Fixed via a "shadow equity" design:** the on/off decision is now driven by a shadow equity curve built from the raw, hypothetical, uninterrupted pre-breaker returns (what the strategy would have earned if never halted), while the actually-realized return series is still `raw_returns × multiplier` — the decision and the realized outcome are computed from two different equity paths, by design, so the breaker can genuinely turn back off. Caught and fixed before any real data was touched, via careful manual tracing of the original logic rather than a failing test.

**Validation:** 16/16 checks passing (up from 15/16 before the fix — the one previously-passing check, `still_above_resume_while_off`, had only been passing as a symptom of the bug, not genuine confirmation, and was corrected alongside the module fix to use shadow-equity drawdown instead of realized drawdown).

## Phase 12c — Combined Signal + Risk Overlay: Full Backtest with Train/Validate Split

**Purpose:** combine Phase 12a's signal with Phase 12b's overlay into one coherent backtest (`trend_meanrev_full_backtest.py`), run on the real 14-asset panel, with a train/validate split for a first look at stability across time.

**Order of operations (pre-committed):** base signal (already lagged, ∈{-1,0,1}) → stop-loss/take-profit overlay (evaluated against the unsized position) → position-size multiplier applied → per-asset P&L → portfolio-level equal-weight aggregation across whichever assets are currently tradeable (a dynamic count N(t), never a pooled fixed-universe cutoff) → portfolio-level drawdown breaker applied once → the same breaker multiplier applied back uniformly to every asset's own P&L.

**Staggered/dynamic tradeable universe:** mirrors this project's standing per-asset-independent principle (Phase 0b onward) — an asset counts toward the portfolio's equal-weight average once its own 252-day trend filter first has a valid value, not from a pooled "youngest asset" cutoff. HYG (2007-04-11 inception) is the last to qualify, ~2008-04. Flagged explicitly: this means the realized portfolio's composition is non-stationary over time (diluting/concentrating as assets come online), the same lesson already learned from the seasonality rule's exposure-fraction dilution.

**Train/validate split:** a single fixed calendar cutoff, **2016-12-31**, not a per-asset split — chosen because the drawdown breaker and portfolio aggregation are inherently portfolio-level constructs that need one shared calendar. This gives even HYG (the latest-qualifying asset) roughly 8 years of TRAIN data and leaves a roughly 9-year VALIDATE window (2017–2025) spanning several real stress periods (the 2018 Q4 selloff, the 2020 COVID crash, the 2022 rate-hike selloff). The signal and overlay are computed once over the full sample and simply sliced for reporting — never reset at the cutoff — to avoid a subtle look-ahead artifact in the breaker's own running peak. **Honestly flagged as a stability check across time, not a strict overfitting-prevention test**, since no parameter was tuned on TRAIN at all (every parameter — the 20-day window, entry/exit z, the 252-day trend lookback, the 15% breaker threshold — was fixed in advance, reused from Bollinger Bands/momentum's own already-validated defaults); an earlier informal "quick look" during the design phase had already glanced at full-sample descriptive numbers before this formal build began.

## Phase 12d — Monte Carlo Permutation Test (Price-Path-Dependent Design)

**Why this rule needed the more expensive MCPT design:** unlike carry trade and seasonality (whose signals never depend on the tested asset's own price path, making a cheap fixed-signal-reshuffle test correct), trend-filtered mean-reversion's trend filter and z-score both depend on the asset's own price history — the same family as momentum and Bollinger Bands. Each trial must reconstruct a synthetic price path from permuted returns and **recompute the entire signal → stop-loss/take-profit → position-sizing chain from scratch**, not just reapply a fixed mask to reshuffled returns (see "Compute Constraints" above, where this exact distinction was first flagged as a general lesson).

**Design (`trend_meanrev_mcpt.py`):** per-asset (14 independent tests); `price_permuted = price_0 × cumprod(1 + permuted_returns)`, leaving each asset's own pre-inception NaN block untouched; trial 0 = real data, NREPS=2000, seed=20260924 (this project's standing convention). **Position-sizing subtlety:** the tested asset's own permuted-path volatility is combined with the OTHER 13 assets' real, fixed historical volatility (computed once, outside the trial loop) to correctly reproduce the true cross-sectional-median-vol sizing mechanism while isolating exactly what's under test — this one asset's own date-to-return relationship — without re-testing the other 13 assets on every trial. **The portfolio-level drawdown breaker is excluded from this per-asset test**, justified two ways: it has no well-defined single-asset analogue, and it engaged on 0.0% of days in the real full-sample backtest (Phase 12c), so including or excluding it is numerically identical either way — verified directly on real SPY and HYG data before relying on the argument.

**Validation (17/17 checks passing):** price-reconstruction correctness (an identity permutation exactly reproduces the original price path; a genuine shuffle preserves the exact return multiset while changing the path); trial-0 consistency against `run_full_strategy`'s own independent per-asset output on a synthetic 3-asset panel (confirmed exact, since the breaker never engages there either); first-tradeable-date invariance under permutation; p-value sanity bounds; and reproducibility (same seed → identical results, different seed → different results). Additionally spot-checked directly on real SPY and HYG data (outside the formal test suite): trial-0 exactly matches `run_full_strategy`'s own output end-to-end, and the real breaker is confirmed to never engage (0.0% of days) on either asset.

**Runtime:** ~0.023–0.03s/trial × 2000 trials × 14 assets ≈ 838 seconds total — exceeding this environment's 10-minute single-command execution cap, so the run was launched as a background process and awaited asynchronously rather than blocking or reducing the trial count below this project's standing 2000-trial convention.

## Phase 12e — Deflated Sharpe Ratio (Mandatory Follow-Up) & Conclusion

**Why this was run:** Phase 12d's result (5 of 14 assets significant, the first multi-asset MCPT result since momentum) is exactly the situation this project's standing rule exists for — MCPT significance across more than one asset is insufficient on its own and requires the DSR follow-up before any edge is accepted as real. Rebuilt fresh as `trend_meanrev_dsr.py` since the original `step6b_deflated_sharpe_ratio.py` file was not present in this session's environment, implementing the identical Bailey & López de Prado (2014) design already validated for momentum (Phase 6b): `expected_max_sharpe` (extreme-value theory, Euler–Mascheroni constant) sets SR0, the N=14-trial multiple-testing-corrected benchmark; the Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio (Lo 2002 / Mertens 2002, using each asset's own T, skewness, and non-excess kurtosis) is evaluated against SR0 for all 14 assets. All PSR/DSR math kept at daily frequency throughout (matching this project's own standing pitfall-avoidance convention); annualized Sharpe reported separately, purely for readability.

**Validation (25/25 checks passing, after two rounds of test-authoring fixes — not module bugs):** `expected_max_sharpe` matched against a hand-derived recomputation; skewness/kurtosis matched against `scipy.stats`' own reference implementation; a Gaussian-returns PSR check against the textbook Lo(2002)/Mertens(2002) formula — **including its `(kurt-1)/4·SR_hat²` term, which does NOT vanish even under perfectly Gaussian returns** (a real, known result about the sample Sharpe ratio's own SR-dependent asymptotic variance, not an approximation this module skips — the test's own first-draft reference formula wrongly omitted this term and had to be corrected); PSR monotonicity and boundary checks; a 40-draw calibration check (a genuinely null 14-asset universe produces a false "survives DSR" verdict 0/40 times); a power check (a genuinely skilled asset embedded among 13 noise assets clearly survives DSR); and full internal-consistency checks between the module's own output and an independent recomputation for all 14 assets.

**0 of 14 assets survive DSR at the 0.95 threshold — a decisive, unambiguous null.** SR0 annualizes to ≈0.927 — *above* even SPY's own strong 0.904 annualized Sharpe, meaning SPY's raw performance doesn't clear the multiple-testing-corrected "expected best of 14" benchmark even before PSR's skew/kurtosis adjustment is applied. Two mechanisms drive this:
1. **Sharpe dispersion inflates SR0.** The 14 trial assets' own daily Sharpes are widely dispersed (annualized: 0.256 for DBC up to 0.904 for SPY, mean ≈0.599) — the wider that spread, the higher `expected_max_sharpe`'s extreme-value correction pushes SR0 above the plain average, precisely because SR0 is answering "how good would the best of 14 independent draws look by pure chance alone," and a widely dispersed set of trial results makes that chance-alone best look better.
2. **Heavy kurtosis penalizes PSR.** Every asset's daily strategy returns show kurtosis far above the Gaussian reference of 3.0 — ranging from 37.5 (TLT) to 206.8 (GLD) — which enters PSR's denominator as `(kurt-1)/4 × SR_hat²` and widens the effective uncertainty band around each asset's own Sharpe, making it harder to distinguish from chance. This heavy-tailed pattern likely reflects the strategy's own return structure (mostly small, diluted returns from the staggered/partial tradeable universe, punctuated by occasional larger moves).

**Conclusion: trend-filtered mean-reversion is concluded null under the project's full two-part standard (MCPT-and-DSR).** This is the **sixth consecutive null primary rule** on this universe — but reached via the single most encouraging-looking intermediate result of any rule tried so far (a 5-of-14 MCPT pass, including one asset, SPY, at p=0.001). A naive stop at the MCPT stage alone would have wrongly reported a real discovery; this is exactly the scenario this project's own "MCPT-and-DSR, not MCPT-alone" rule exists to catch, and the clearest real-data illustration of that rule's value produced by this project to date.

**Key coordination rules, already agreed and carried forward across all rules tried so far:**
- An edge is only accepted as real if **both** the permutation test and the Deflated Sharpe Ratio are significant — either alone is insufficient. (Exception, explicitly reasoned through: Bollinger Bands' descriptive result was decisive enough in the negative direction that this bar was judged unreachable and the formal tests were skipped by user decision — see above. Seasonality's and carry trade's per-asset MCPTs each came back uniformly unremarkable, so neither ever reached the point of needing a DSR-style pooled correction. **Trend-filtered mean-reversion (Phase 12) is the clearest real-data illustration yet of why this rule requires BOTH stages: its MCPT alone (5/14 significant, including SPY at p=0.001) looked like the strongest result since momentum, but its DSR came back decisively null (0/14 survive) — exactly the false-positive risk this two-part standard exists to catch.**)
- The permutation test scrambles each asset's own return sequence independently (destroying its autocorrelation) while preserving cross-asset relationships — not a joint/paired shuffle. (The value factor's MCPT is the one exception to this shape, by design — see Phase 11 for why a cross-sectional relabeling scheme fits its cross-sectional null hypothesis better than a time-series shuffle would.)
- Walkforward testing alone prevents look-ahead leakage but does **not** address selection bias, multiple-testing inflation, or label overlap — hence MCPT/DSR plus purged CV for any meta-labeling.
- A meta-labeling feature search is capped at a small, pre-committed number of rounds agreed in advance — not an open-ended search. (Time-series momentum used exactly two rounds, seven features, both null — that discipline carries forward to whatever rule comes next.)
- Any replacement rule still goes through the same significance-testing gauntlet (MCPT/DSR) — simplicity of computation is not a rigor exemption, *unless* (as with Bollinger Bands) the descriptive result is decisive enough on its own that the formal test cannot change the conclusion — a distinction to draw carefully and explicitly, not casually, if it comes up again for a future rule.
- A full-sample "pick the single best result" framing is itself a form of data mining, whatever stage of the pipeline it happens at (an asset, a lookback, a pair, or a calendar-effect variant) — the fix is always a pre-committed, point-in-time-correct selection rule evaluated on its own aggregate results, not a hand-picked best case. (Seasonality applied this directly: month-end was picked over Sell-in-May/January Barometer BEFORE any real-data run, on power/generality grounds stated in advance, not after seeing which looked best.)
- The same data-snooping symmetry applies in reverse — excluding specific results (windows, periods, assets) *after* seeing which ones helped or hurt is just as much data mining as hand-picking the single best one, even when the excluded cases have an independently plausible-sounding story. The only honest resolution is to either commit to an exclusion rule *before* seeing outcomes, or test the complete, as-built result exactly as it stands. (The equity-index-concentration pattern observed in Phase 12d's MCPT results is deliberately NOT being used this way — see Phase 12d.)
- MACD was ruled out from consideration (not even built) purely on hypothesis-family grounds, before any data was touched — it shares momentum's already-null trend-following hypothesis, so testing it would risk re-litigating a settled question rather than asking a genuinely new one. Ruling out a candidate rule on this basis, before building it, is itself a legitimate and useful application of this project's overall skepticism. (The same reasoning was applied again when seasonality's candidates were surveyed: Multiple Time Frames was ruled out on the same grounds — stripped of its packaging, it's momentum's hypothesis re-sliced by horizon.)
- **A statistics function that silently defaults its degrees-of-freedom (`ddof`) differently depending on whether it's called on a pandas Series or a plain numpy array is a real, easy-to-miss bug source** — caught this round via a trial-0 cross-consistency check between a backtest module and its own MCPT module; the general fix (pass `ddof` explicitly, never rely on a type's own default) is now this project's standing practice for any shared statistics helper.
- **A single data panel used for two different purposes (valuation vs. P&L) needs two different treatments of price** — a raw traded price level for anything valuation/market-cap-related (what a share is actually worth right now), and a dividend/split-adjusted total-return series for anything P&L-related (what a holder actually earned). The value factor's `price_panel`/`total_ret_panel` split (Phase 11) is the first time this project needed both from the same underlying instrument simultaneously, but the underlying principle is the same one behind Phase 0's original total-return-index construction — now generalized as standing practice for any future individual-stock module.
- **A portfolio-level risk control's own on/off decision must never be driven by the equity path it itself has already altered** — Phase 12b's circuit-breaker bug (deciding from realized, already breaker-cut equity, which can never recover on its own) is a specific instance of a more general trap: any control that both observes and modifies the same series risks becoming self-referentially stuck. The fix (a "shadow" equity path built from the raw, hypothetical, unconstrained series purely for the on/off decision, kept separate from the actually-realized, controlled series) is now this project's standing pattern for any future portfolio-level risk control.

## Open Methodology Watch-Items

- **Universe size vs. HRP comparison:** resolved in favor of doing the comparison at 14 assets rather than 4 — the covariance matrix is meaningfully richer, which should let HRP and plain risk parity actually differentiate in Phase 8.
- **Transaction costs:** not yet modeled anywhere in the pipeline — explicit scope limitation, applies to whatever primary rule is chosen next. Directly relevant to the pairs rule's spread rule and to Bollinger Bands, both of which can generate frequent entries/exits (Bollinger Bands' short-day counts in particular — 1,093 to 2,010 per asset — imply a great many round trips; transaction costs were never modeled before the rule was concluded null on gross returns, so it's possible net-of-cost performance is even worse, not better, than reported here). Seasonality's ~12 round-trips/year/asset is comparatively cheap by contrast, but moot given the rule concluded null on gross returns already. The value factor's annual rebalance is the cheapest turnover profile in the project so far (30 stocks, once a year), but still unmodeled. **Trend-filtered mean-reversion (Phase 12) reuses Bollinger Bands' own entry/exit frequency (hundreds of long/short days per asset), so it inherits the same unmodeled-cost caveat — moot now that the rule is concluded null on gross returns, but worth remembering if a similar mean-reversion design is ever revisited.**
- **Shorting costs:** any long/short rule implies short positions; borrow costs not modeled, same category as above — particularly relevant to Bollinger Bands given how short-side-heavy its trading was (14/14 assets more short-days than long-days) and how much of its negative result traces to the short leg specifically. Not applicable to seasonality (long-only) or carry trade's single-asset long/short-only-in-aggregate design; the value factor's short leg (10 of 30 stocks, annually) is a new instance of this same unmodeled cost. Trend-filtered mean-reversion also trades both directions (gated by trend), so the same caveat applies, again moot given the null conclusion.
- **Microstructural features (`Chapter_19.py`, AFML):** would require an additional WRDS pull for intraday High/Low data not currently in the pipeline; deferred unless a specific need arises. (Distinct from Kaufman TSaM's own Ch. 19, "Multiple Time Frames," which was surveyed and explicitly ruled out — see the seasonality strategic-decision section above.)
- **Purged-CV purge width (from Phase 3b, general lesson for next time):** 1-month purge/embargo covered direct holding-window overlap but not the full feature-construction lookback (up to 12 months for some features). Worth deciding deliberately (not defaulting) if meta-labeling is ever revisited for a surviving rule — **including if meta-labeling is pursued for trend-filtered mean-reversion's own bets as a conditional-edge test (see "Next Step" below); this rule's 252-day trend-filter lookback is longer than momentum's own longest lookback (12 months) that motivated the original purge-width caveat.**
- **Fixed-lag ADF approximation (pairs rule, concluded):** the pairs-rule MCPTs used a fixed ADF lag rather than AIC-searched, for tractability at 150-200+ trials — the confirmed explanation for why `step3b`'s own trial-0 (real-data) Sharpe/cumulative return (0.047 / 2.30%) didn't match Phase 3a's original AIC-lag descriptive numbers (0.207 / 34.0%). Moot now that pairs trading is concluded; not applicable to Bollinger Bands, seasonality, carry, the value factor, or trend-filtered mean-reversion, none of which has a lag-selection step at all.
- **Entry/exit threshold choice:** entry_z=2.0 / exit_z=0.5 were adopted as standard textbook values (Gatev-Goetzmann-Rouwenhorst for pairs trading, Bollinger's own convention for the Bollinger rule), not tuned on this data — deliberately, to avoid an extra layer of researcher-degrees-of-freedom, and reused unchanged again for the trend-filtered mean-reversion rule's own trigger (Phase 12a). The 252-day trend-filter lookback and the 15% drawdown-breaker threshold were likewise fixed in advance rather than tuned. Worth flagging explicitly if any of these thresholds is ever revisited in a future adapted variant.
- **Crisis-driven shared-shock clustering (pairs rule, concluded):** observed at TWO levels of the pipeline (screening-stage clustering around systemic-stress episodes; portfolio-level concentration in two crisis-driven windows) — the same underlying mechanism (a single dominant shock inflating a formation window's variance, making many unrelated pairs look related together and straining FDR's independence assumptions). Resolved for pairs trading via the full-pipeline MCPT; worth watching for in any future formation-window-based rule on this universe, including the value factor's own annual formation windows.
- **RESOLVED — step6's reusability corrected to a general lesson:** `step6_mcpt_permutation_test.py`'s documented design specifically re-runs a "search N=3,6,12, pick the best Sharpe" procedure per permuted trial. Originally flagged as an open question for Bollinger Bands (never answered — that rule was concluded null on the descriptive result alone), it has now been answered definitively by seasonality, carry, the value factor, and trend-filtered mean-reversion alike: step6's literal code is NOT reusable for a rule without its own internal search, and in practice has not been literally reused by ANY rule after momentum. Every subsequent rule needs its own dedicated module built on the same design principles — and, per Phase 12d, whether that module is CHEAP (no price-path dependence, e.g. carry/seasonality) or EXPENSIVE (full-mechanics rerun per trial, e.g. momentum/Bollinger Bands/trend-filtered mean-reversion) depends specifically on whether the signal depends on the tested asset's own price path.
- **Short-leg-specific failure mode (Bollinger Bands, concluded):** the diagnosed mechanism (shorting "overbought" conditions in persistently-trending-up assets) is specific to a *two-sided* contrarian rule on assets with strong secular drift. **This was directly tested by Phase 12's trend filter, which suppresses exactly this counter-trend case — the descriptive result (Phase 12c) shows some assets' strategy Sharpe now exceeding buy-and-hold (e.g. SPY 0.904 vs. 0.634), consistent with the fix addressing the diagnosed mechanism at the descriptive level — but the rule still fails the formal MCPT-and-DSR standard overall (Phase 12e), so the trend filter's apparent descriptive improvement over Bollinger Bands does not, on its own, constitute a validated edge.**
- **Exposure-fraction dilution of Sharpe (seasonality, concluded):** any rule that is only partially invested (flat 0-return days mixed into the strategy's own return series) will mechanically show a lower annualized Sharpe than buy-and-hold even under a pure chance null with no real edge — roughly by a factor of `sqrt(exposure fraction)` for small daily means relative to daily volatility. A raw "beats buy-and-hold on Sharpe" headline is therefore a weaker, more easily misread signal for any future partially-invested rule (a seasonal tilt, a regime filter, a vol-targeting overlay) than it was for the fully-invested rules tried so far — always compute and report the exposure fraction alongside any such comparison, as `season_step3a_backtest.py` now does. **Trend-filtered mean-reversion is also only partially invested (long/short/flat, gated further by the trend filter), so any future refinement of its own descriptive comparison should report exposure fraction explicitly, the same caveat.**
- **A shared statistics helper must not rely on a type's own `ddof` default:** `ret.std()` defaults to `ddof=1` (sample) for a pandas Series but `ddof=0` (population) for a numpy array. A function called on both types (as `_ann_sharpe` now is, across several modules' backtest/MCPT pairs) will silently disagree with itself unless `ddof` is passed explicitly. Caught via a trial-0 cross-consistency check, not by inspection — a good argument for always including that specific check (the MCPT module's "real" statistic must exactly match the descriptive backtest's own number) whenever a permutation-test module is built on top of an existing backtest module. This exact check is what's now guaranteed structurally, not just by convention, in the value factor's `value_mcpt.py` via its shared `_bm_at_formation`/`_holding_window` helpers, and again in `trend_meanrev_mcpt.py`/`trend_meanrev_dsr.py`'s own consistency checks against `trend_meanrev_full_backtest.py`.
- **"restrict-then-shift" bug pattern (carry trade):** computing a lag/shift on a signal AFTER restricting it to an intersection window (rather than shifting the signal's own full timeline first, then intersecting) can silently zero out genuine information at the boundary of the restricted window — caught in `carry_backtest.py` via an independent-recomputation test check, fixed by always shifting the full series first. Worth checking for in any future lagged-signal design.
- **duration-dependent carry sign (concluded for this specific signal):** a single term-spread signal (DGS20-DGS3MO) that is mechanically correct for a long-duration bond (TLT) is directionally backwards for a short-duration one (SHY) — an inverted curve is bad for long duration but favorable for short duration, since short-duration income tracks short rates directly. Any future carry-style rule spanning multiple durations needs either a duration-matched signal per asset or an explicit split, never a single spread applied uniformly.
- **a synthetic test fixture must span far enough back for its own lookback logic, not just the window under test (value factor):** `test_value_backtest.py`'s price panel originally started exactly at the first formation year, leaving no valid reference date for that formation's point-in-time market-equity lookback (which needs Dec 31 of the *prior* year) — silently collapsing the first period to an all-flat fallback rather than erroring. The general lesson (already seen in different forms with Bollinger Bands and pairs trading): a synthetic fixture's date range must be checked against every lookback/reference-date computation the module under test actually performs, not just against the headline window being tested.
- **CRSP ticker-symbol reuse vs. genuine corporate-identity change require different handling, and neither should be guessed (value factor):** resolving the Dow-30's 10 ambiguous ticker→permno mappings surfaced two distinct situations that look identical at first glance (multiple permnos share a ticker string) but demand opposite treatment — an unrelated, long-defunct company that happened to hold the same ticker decades ago (7 of 10 cases) simply gets excluded by picking the active/current permno, while a genuine merger/identity change (HON, JPM, TRV) still gets only ONE permno going forward, with the pre-merger entity's history deliberately not spliced in, consistent with this project's no-fabricated-continuity principle. Both require inspecting issuer names and active date ranges directly — never inferring from the ticker or permno count alone.
- **a date-aware join is required whenever a link table's own validity window can change over time (value factor):** the CCM link table's 8 permnos with two linked GVKEYs could have been either a benign historical GVKEY reassignment or a genuine duplicate; an unconditional join on GVKEY alone cannot tell the difference. The general fix — restrict the join using the link table's own `linkdt`/`linkenddt` window against each fundamental row's own date, then explicitly check for any remaining multi-value conflict — is now `value_data_prep.py`'s standing pattern and is reusable for any future join through a similarly time-varying identifier-mapping table.
- **NEW — a portfolio-level control's on/off decision must use a "shadow," unconstrained equity path, never the path it has already modified (trend-filtered mean-reversion, Phase 12b):** see the dedicated "Key coordination rules" entry above for the general statement; recorded here as a watch-item too since it's the kind of bug that can silently pass a test suite built around the same flawed assumption (the one previously-passing check that had to be corrected alongside the fix, `still_above_resume_while_off`, illustrates exactly this risk).
- **NEW — a strong multi-asset MCPT result is not sufficient on its own; only the live real-data case fully demonstrates why (trend-filtered mean-reversion, Phase 12):** every rule before this one that reached the MCPT stage came back either uniformly null (seasonality, carry) or was skipped entirely (Bollinger Bands) — none had previously produced a genuinely strong multi-asset MCPT pass to test the DSR follow-up against in practice, since momentum. Phase 12 is the first real-data case since momentum itself where the two-stage standard actually did its job: a 5-of-14, p=0.001-at-the-best-asset MCPT result that would have been reported as a discovery under an MCPT-only standard, decisively reversed by DSR. Worth citing as the canonical real-data example whenever this project's own two-part rule is questioned or revisited.
- **NEW — a circuit breaker that never engages on real data is not evidence against its design, but should be reported as such, not silently omitted (trend-filtered mean-reversion, Phase 12c):** the portfolio drawdown breaker built for this rule never triggered in any window on real data (0.0% of days halted, full/train/validate alike) — pure unused insurance for this particular strategy's actual realized drawdown profile. This is worth remembering if the breaker's own trigger threshold (15%) or design is ever reused for a future, more volatile rule where it might actually bind — its correctness there would need to be checked directly (as Phase 12b's own synthetic and hand-built formula tests did), not assumed from this rule's own quiet real-data experience.
- **NEW — equity-index concentration in Phase 12d's MCPT result, explicitly unconfirmed:** the 5 (borderline 6, counting QQQ) MCPT-significant assets are exactly the universe's broad equity-index/equity-adjacent-credit ETFs, with every non-equity asset non-significant. Flagged as an observation worth a fresh, independent test if pursued further (e.g. as a conditional-edge/meta-labeling feature, or as the basis for a deliberately narrower future universe stated and justified BEFORE seeing new data) — never as a retroactive justification for narrowing the universe post-hoc, which this project's own repeated experience with post-hoc selection (SHY/DSR, the pairs-rule COVID-window disqualification) warns against.
- **Primary-rule search history:** six rules concluded null (time-series momentum, statistical-arbitrage pairs trading, Bollinger Bands mean-reversion, seasonality/month-end effect, single-asset carry trade, and trend-filtered mean-reversion with a built-in risk overlay) — momentum, pairs trading, seasonality, carry, and trend-filtered mean-reversion via formal MCPT/DSR-family testing; Bollinger Bands via a decisive descriptive result alone. ## Phase C, Part 1 — Unhedged Baseline Portfolio

**Purpose:** before any options mechanics are built, establish the exact counterfactual the hedge will be judged against — a plain, fully-invested, equal-weighted, buy-and-hold portfolio across the same 14-asset universe, using no signal at all.

**Design (`optionshedge_benchmark.py`):** reuses the staggered-universe, dynamic-N(t) equal-weight aggregation principle established in Phase 0b and reused in Phase 12c, but strips out every other piece of machinery — no trend filter, no stop-loss, no position sizing, no drawdown breaker, no execution lag (there is no signal being decided from market data to lag). Every asset is long at 100% of an equal share from its own first tradeable date (its first *defined return*, not merely its first price level — the same one-day distinction the seasonality rule's Phase 3a had to fix) onward. ## Phase C, Part 2 — Protective-Put Overlay Mechanics

**Purpose:** build and validate the put-pricing/rolling mechanics themselves (`optionshedge_overlay.py`), independent of any real OptionMetrics data, so the module is ready the moment the real pull comes back.

**Design (pre-committed, confirmed with the user):** 25-delta puts, 30-calendar-day tenor, rolled at expiration (the CBOE PPUT-style monthly cadence, adopted as an externally-documented standard rather than tuned on this data). Each roll, the strike is solved by inverting the Black-Scholes put-delta formula for the strike giving delta = -0.25 at that date's spot/implied-vol/tenor (`solve_strike_for_target_delta`, via bracketing root-find, since put delta is monotonic in strike). Once struck, the put is repriced daily via Black-Scholes using that day's actual spot and the option's own shrinking time-to-expiry, **holding implied volatility fixed at its value on the roll date** — an explicitly flagged "sticky-strike" simplification (real implied vol moves over an option's life; this model doesn't claim otherwise, it's a standard, recognized simplification for this style of backtest, not a hidden approximation).

**Genuine design catch, self-identified before any real data was touched (the same class of catch as Phase 11's raw-price/total-return split):** an option's strike and payoff are denominated in the underlying's actual TRADED price, never a dividend-reinvested total-return index level — using this project's usual TRI panel as if it were tradeable spot would silently misprice every strike and payoff. The module therefore takes two separate price series per asset, exactly mirroring Phase 11's `price_panel`/`total_ret_panel` split: `raw_price_panel` (actual traded price, feeds ONLY the option strike/payoff/mark-to-market) and `total_ret_panel` (dividend-reinvested TRI, feeds ONLY the underlying leg's own P&L).

**Roll-day accounting subtlety (found and correctly resolved while building the test suite):** on a roll date, the day's booked return uses the OUTGOING put's own settlement value (Black-Scholes at T=0, which collapses exactly to intrinsic value) relative to yesterday's held value; a NEW put is then struck at its own fair value (a zero-NPV purchase, no same-day P&L), becoming the cost basis for the following day. Consequence: `put_value_panel` stores the value of whatever put is held at the END of each date, which means it CANNOT be exactly reconstructed by naively differencing consecutive entries on a roll date — a first-draft test check assumed it could and failed; correctly diagnosed as a test-design flaw (not a module bug, since the module's own return calculation was economically correct throughout) and fixed by replacing that check with a genuine, independently-written, from-scratch parallel reimplementation of the full roll/mark/return logic.

**Validation (synthetic data, 26/26 checks passing):** Black-Scholes put price against a known textbook reference value and correct T→0 intrinsic-value convergence (both ITM and OTM); put delta matching a numerical finite-difference derivative of the price function; the strike solver's round-trip accuracy across four target deltas (-0.10 to -0.50); the raw-price/total-return separation (an engineered synthetic panel with a deliberate constant dividend drag confirms the module correctly draws each input from the right series); a "put never pays off" scenario (a steady low-vol uptrend shows the hedged path trailing the unhedged path by a small, persistent premium drag, highly correlated with it); a "put cushions a crash" scenario (an engineered 35% crash shows the hedged path's max drawdown at roughly a third of the unhedged path's own); roll-timing sanity (~30-day spacing over the sample); and the from-scratch parallel reimplementation matching both `hedged_returns` and `put_value_panel` exactly.

**Output artifact:** none yet — this module has not been run on real data, since it requires OptionMetrics' actual 25-delta/30-day implied volatility surface, which this project's sandbox cannot pull itself (the same WRDS access limitation as every prior real-data phase).

**WRDS pull handed to the user (`wrds_optionshedge_pull.py`):** requests OptionMetrics' standardized Volatility Surface at the fixed 30-day/25-delta put point for each of the 14 tickers, resolved from CRSP permno to OptionMetrics secid via an official WRDS-maintained linking table (the same "resolve identifiers via an official link table, then verify" pattern as Phase 11's CCM join) — reusing this project's ALREADY-PULLED raw CRSP price for the strike/payoff mechanics rather than a second, separately-sourced price series. **Flagged explicitly, more prominently than most prior pull scripts in this project:** OptionMetrics/IvyDB schema and table names have varied across WRDS's own versions, and this script has not been run or verified against the user's actual subscription — it should be checked against the user's own WRDS schema browser (table names, and specifically whether `delta` is stored as an integer percent or a decimal) before trusting its results.

## Phase C, Part 3 — Real-Data Run & Conclusion

**Data received:** the user re-surfaced the original Phase 0b raw CRSP pull (`crsp_daily_raw.csv` + `crsp_daily_raw_expansion.csv` — never previously persisted as a standalone file beyond building the TRI panel) and ran the real WRDS OptionMetrics Volatility Surface pull (30-day/25-delta puts) via WRDS's web-query wizard, working through it live with screenshot-by-screenshot guidance (category selection — Volatility Surface, not Historical Volatility; ticker- vs. secid-format company codes; Option Type = Put Only; the Conditional Statement Builder for `days=30`/`delta=-25`).

**First real issue found and fixed — secid-to-ticker identity:** the first pull came back with 15 unique secids instead of 14 and no `ticker` column. Re-running the same query with `ticker`/`cusip` added as output fields resolved it cleanly: the extra secid (`8274`, data spanning only 1996-01-04 to 1997-04-30) was tagged `ticker=GLD` but with cusip `80217610` — not the real SPDR Gold Shares cusip (`78463V10`, secid `122392`) — confirming, via hard identifier evidence rather than inferred dates, the same CRSP ticker-reuse pattern already resolved in Phase 11 (an unrelated security briefly held the "GLD" ticker string in 1996-97, decades before the ETF existed). Secid 8274 was excluded; the remaining 15 rows resolved to exactly 14 tickers, one secid each, no duplicates or gaps.

**Second real issue found and fixed — split-adjustment (more consequential, caught by a results sanity check, not assumed in advance):** CRSP's `dlyprc` is the literal nominal price on each date, not adjusted across a stock split — a split is a corporate action, not a price move, but it shows up as a genuine single-day price cliff in `dlyprc` even though `dlyret` that same day correctly reflects the true (tiny) economic return. Cross-checked directly against CRSP's own `dlycumfacpr` (cumulative price-adjustment factor, which steps down by exactly the split ratio on each split date), six real splits exist across the universe: QQQ 2000-03-20 (2:1), EFA/EEM/IWM 2005-06-09 (3:1/3:1/2:1), EEM/SLV 2008-07-24 (3:1/10:1). Feeding literal `dlyprc` into the overlay as "raw traded price" made a put struck before a split look, mechanically, like it went enormously in the money right after — not from a real crash, but because the held strike (a fixed dollar figure) was never rescaled for the new, smaller nominal share price. This was caught, not assumed: a `put_value_panel` sanity check on the first real run showed a handful of puts apparently worth 100-190% of the underlying's own current price, and every one of those dates landed exactly on one of the six known split dates — including SLV's 2008-07-24 split, which fell inside the 2008 GFC stress window and had inflated the hedge's apparent benefit there specifically. **Fixed** by feeding the overlay a split-adjusted (but still not dividend-adjusted) continuous price series, `dlyprc / dlycumfacpr`, rather than literal `dlyprc` — mirroring how a real listed option's own strike is adjusted at each split by exchange convention, while keeping the raw-price/total-return distinction this module's design already draws (splits are corporate mechanics an option tracks; dividends are not built into a put's payoff). Re-verified clean afterward: no single-day move in the split-adjusted panel exceeds 30% anywhere across all 14 assets' full histories, and every remaining large `put_value_panel` reading now lands on a genuine, well-known crisis date (2008-10-24/27, 2020-03-18/19/23, 2001-04-04, 2013-04-15's real single-day gold crash, 2022-06-14) rather than an adjustment artifact.

**Coverage-matching (a real design point, not a nicety):** each asset's options data starts well after both its ETF inception and its raw-price/TRI history (e.g. SPY's price history runs from 1993 but its 25-delta/30-day options data only starts 2005-01-10 in this pull; every asset's options-derived series is capped at 2025-08-29, a WRDS data-availability limit already flagged in this environment). `raw_price_panel` and `total_ret_panel` were masked, per asset, to each asset's own options-data window before running the overlay — which, as a side effect, makes the unhedged comparison series automatically coverage-matched to the hedged one (same dates, same assets), isolating the hedge's actual effect rather than conflating it with a different, later sample period. The resulting portfolio-level comparison spans 1999-03-11 to 2025-08-29 (~26.5 years, from QQQ's options-data start, the earliest across the universe).

**Real-data result:**

| Statistic | Unhedged (matched window) | Hedged (protective put) |
|---|---|---|
| Annualized Sharpe | 0.399 | 0.199 |
| Annualized return | 7.65% | 2.62% |
| Cumulative return | 346.8% | 58.4% |
| Max drawdown | 70.2% | 64.0% |
| 5%-tail daily CVaR | -2.95% | -2.02% |
| 1%-tail daily CVaR | -4.79% | -3.30% |
| Worst single day | -10.39% | -7.12% |

**Stress-window comparison (the three windows Part 1 already flagged):**

| Window | Unhedged: max DD / cum. return | Hedged: max DD / cum. return |
|---|---|---|
| 2008 GFC (2008-01 to 2009-06) | 34.0% / -11.2% | 22.2% / -6.7% |
| 2020 COVID (2020-01 to 2020-06) | 21.4% / +0.9% | 7.8% / +3.5% |
| 2022 rate-hike selloff (2022-01 to 2022-12) | 19.6% / -13.4% | 16.3% / -12.4% |

**Interpretation — a genuine risk/return trade-off, not a null result:** the protective put does exactly what insurance is supposed to do, best in a fast, V-shaped shock — in 2020 COVID it cut max drawdown by nearly two-thirds AND finished the window with a *better* cumulative return than unhedged, since the put's payoff during the crash outran the premiums paid before and after it. It helps more modestly in a slower-moving crisis (2008 GFC, 2022): a real reduction in drawdown, but a 30-day put can expire worthless several times over during a grinding, months-long decline, capturing less of the total damage. Across the full matched history, tail risk falls on every measure (CVaR at both tails, worst single day, max drawdown) — but the **continuous cost of buying a new 25-delta put every month for 26+ years is a severe drag on compounded wealth**: the hedged portfolio ends with roughly a sixth of the unhedged portfolio's terminal cumulative return, and its annualized Sharpe is *lower*, not higher, than the unhedged portfolio's, because a persistent insurance premium reduces average return by more than it reduces return variance in Sharpe's own terms. This is a well-documented real-world property of continuously-held protective-put strategies — CBOE's own PPUT index has a long history of underperforming buy-and-hold for the same structural reason — now independently reproduced on this project's own 14-asset universe.

**Why no MCPT/DSR follow-up here:** unlike every primary-rule phase above, Phase C was never a test of whether an edge is statistically real — the put overlay is not claimed to generate positive expected return; it is explicitly, by design, a cost paid for reduced risk. The relevant question is the descriptive risk/return comparison above (does it reduce drawdown and tail risk, at what cost to return), not a null hypothesis a permutation test could attack.

## Phase D — Market-Breadth Divergence Rule (Designed, Built, Synthetic-Validated)

**Origin:** the user's own idea, raised directly in conversation — S&P 500 "stocks above moving average" breadth indicators (10/20/50/100/200-day variants) as a market-condition signal: when the index makes a new high but participation underneath it is fading, that divergence could flag a shift toward a more conservative regime, usable to adjust existing strategies' positions/direction. Asked directly whether this could help the six null primary rules.

**Why this is a genuinely different hypothesis, not a rehash:** every rule tried so far (Phases 2–12, Phase C) is derived from either the 14-asset panel's own price history or one external rate series. Breadth is a cross-sectional, market-wide internals measure built from ~500 *other* companies — the real thing this project's own "Strategic Decision — Choosing a Fourth Primary Rule" section had once flagged and set aside ("breadth is a market-wide construct built for hundreds of securities; reinterpreting it across only 14 ETFs was assessed as too statistically thin to be a credible test"). Using the actual S&P 500's breadth, rather than trying to fake it on the 14-ETF universe, directly resolves that earlier objection.

**Scoping decision (explicit user choice):** rather than graft the idea onto an already-tested null rule — which would mean picking the target rule *after* knowing which one it might rescue, exactly the post-hoc selection risk this project's whole discipline exists to catch — the user chose to test it as **a new, standalone Rule #7 against plain SPY buy-and-hold**, through the identical backtest → MCPT protocol every other rule has gone through. A rule-agnostic overlay applied across all six existing rules, and a conditioning overlay grafted onto one pre-chosen existing rule, were both offered and declined in favor of this cleaner option.

**Design (pre-committed via two explicit rounds of user confirmation, before any real data was pulled or examined):**
- **Breadth measure: % of S&P 500 constituents above their own 200-day MA — one window only**, not the full 10/20/50/100/200 family the user's original idea named. Using all five and reporting whichever diverges most would repeat momentum's own Phase 6a search-and-select risk. 200-day is the single most standard, most-cited long-term breadth reading. The full 5-window family is a flagged, not-yet-built Part 2 extension, reusing momentum's already-validated search-and-correct MCPT design if this version shows something worth extending.
- **Trigger: SPY at a new 252-trading-day (~1yr) closing high, AND breadth's own trailing trend is falling** (a raw sign check, `breadth_pct.diff(20) < 0` — no fitted threshold). The 20-day window is reused from Bollinger Bands'/trend-filtered mean-reversion's own already-established rolling window, for internal consistency rather than searched on this rule's own data. Two alternative trigger definitions (comparing breadth across successive price peaks; an absolute breadth threshold) were raised and declined in favor of this one, for having no tuned-looking parameter.
- **Position: long (+1) by default, FLAT (0) — never short — when a divergence is flagged.** The user's original framing raised long/short as an option; going short would add a second, harder-to-defend claim on top of the first. A short-leg extension is a flagged Part 2 if the flat version shows something real.
- **Raw-price-vs-total-return split, reused directly from Phase 11/Phase C:** the "new high" check uses SPY's RAW (split-adjusted) price — a literal chart-pattern construct — while the strategy's own realized P&L uses SPY's TOTAL-RETURN index. Both series already exist in this project (`raw_price_panel_14assets.csv`, `tri_full_panel_14assets.csv`) — **no new SPY price data is needed at all.** The only genuinely new data this rule requires is the breadth panel itself.
- **1-day execution lag**, this project's standing convention.

**A genuine design correction, caught before any code was written:** unlike carry trade's signal, which never touches TLT's own price at all, this signal's "new high" half is computed from SPY's OWN price path. That disqualifies it from carry/seasonality's cheap "permute returns, reapply one fixed signal mask" MCPT design — a permuted SPY price path has different rolling maxima, so the new-high flag must be recomputed per trial. The final design is a **hybrid** of two already-validated patterns: trend-filtered mean-reversion's price-path-reconstruction approach (Phase 12d) for the new-high half, combined with carry trade's fixed-external-signal approach for the breadth-trend half (built from ~500 other companies, never permuted). The permutation itself draws one shared random reordering of trading-day positions and applies it identically to both SPY's raw-price returns and its TRI returns, preserving the real same-day pairing between the two while breaking the temporal relationship under test.

**Modules built and synthetic-validated this session (62/62 checks passing across four modules, zero real data touched yet):**

One test-authoring bug caught and fixed while writing the suite (not a module bug): `.diff(n)`'s first valid position is one index later than `.rolling(n).max()`'s own `min_periods` convention — a real, easy-to-miss off-by-one between the two pandas methods, now documented in the test file's own comments.

## Phase E — Capital Allocation (Pillar 3: EW / IVP / HRP / CLA-GMV)

**Reused directly, per standing "use only once flagged" practice:** `Chapter_16.py` (the HRP paper's implementation, via Charles Rambo's companion code) and `CLA.py`/`CLA_Main.py`/`CLA_Data.csv` (Bailey & López de Prado's own Critical Line Algorithm, from davidhbailey.com) — supplied by the user earlier in the project and untouched until flagged into this phase's design. No new data pull was needed at all.

**Design (pre-committed):**
- **Four allocators**, sharing one `(cov, corr) -> weights` interface: **EW** (1/N, the naive baseline); **IVP** (naive risk parity / inverse-variance, ignoring off-diagonal covariance — what the roadmap's "Inverse Vol / Risk Parity" line refers to); **HRP** (López de Prado 2016 — single-linkage clustering on correlation-distance, quasi-diagonalization, recursive bisection); **CLA-GMV** (the Critical Line Algorithm's global-minimum-variance turning point — deliberately NOT a max-Sharpe tangency portfolio, sidestepping the return-forecasting problem this project's own six null results already argue against trusting; the supplied reference code's own convention of using an arbitrary placeholder mean to reach this one portfolio was kept).
- **252-day trailing estimation window, calendar month-end rebalancing** — reusing this project's own established windows/conventions (Phase 2's monthly rebalance, the 252-day window shared by momentum/trend-filtered mean-reversion/breadth) over the reference code's own illustrative synthetic-demo parameters.
- **Dynamic, per-asset-independent eligible universe**: an asset counts toward a given rebalance only once it has 252 valid trailing daily returns ending on that date (the same `min_periods` convention as `compute_above_200dma_panel`), mirroring Phase 0b/12c's staggered-inception principle; a rebalance with fewer than 2 eligible assets is skipped.
- **1-day execution lag**, this project's standing convention.
- `compute_risk_stats` (Phase C) reused for evaluation — a risk-reduction claim is judged on drawdown/CVaR alongside Sharpe, not Sharpe alone.

**A genuine bug found and fixed in the supplied CLA reference implementation, verified rather than assumed:** on a poorly-conditioned covariance matrix (near-duplicate, >0.99-correlated assets), the turning-point algorithm can produce an intermediate point that violates its own box constraint, which the algorithm's own purge step then correctly discards — but that can take the entire remainder of the true efficient path down with it, leaving only a far-from-optimal corner solution. Confirmed this is a property of the **original, unmodified** `CLA.py` itself (reproduced byte-for-byte on the same synthetic panel, not introduced by this project's port) via an independent SLSQP solve showing the true long-only minimum-variance portfolio was materially better (variance 0.504) than the degenerate path's result (3.99). **Fix:** every `get_CLA()` call now checks its own result against the trivial Equal-Weight benchmark (always feasible, so a genuine global minimum can never be worse) and falls back to a direct constrained QP solve when CLA's own path fails that check, logged via a module-level counter so a real run can report how often this triggers rather than hide it.

**Synthetic validation (46/46 checks across three modules):**
- `portfolio_allocators.py` (19/19) — basic per-allocator correctness (weights sum to 1, IVP/HRP/CLA hand-computable and clustering-correctness cases), plus a reproduction of Chapter_16.py's own `hrp_MC` synthetic experiment (rho=0.95 correlated/redundant assets): this reimplementation shows the same qualitative ordering as the published finding (HRP's out-of-sample outcome variance across 200 trials lower than both IVP's and CLA-GMV's).
- `pillar3_walkforward.py` (16/16) — no look-ahead (weights at a rebalance date provably unaffected by perturbing later prices), correct staggered-inception eligibility timing, correct 1-day-lag application, and a hand-computed EW check.
- `pillar3_significance.py` (11/11) — see below.

**Significance test — a new design, flagged explicitly before writing any code:** none of this project's existing MCPT designs apply, since there is no directional bet here to question (every eligible asset is held long, always, under all four schemes) — the actual hypothesis is "does the real cross-sectional correlation structure between these 14 assets let a smarter allocator achieve genuinely lower risk than naive Equal Weight." Rather than a full independent reshuffle of each asset's returns (which would also destroy each asset's own autocorrelation/volatility clustering, conflating two different effects), the null permutes via an independent random **circular shift** of each asset's own valid return history — preserving that asset's own marginal distribution and time-series behavior exactly, while surgically destroying only the cross-sectional pairing across assets on a given date. Test statistic: realized annualized-volatility reduction relative to EW. Power-checked on synthetic data: significant (p=0.01) when real correlation/redundancy is present, correctly non-significant (p≈0.92-0.95) when it is not.

**Real-data result (full sample, 396 monthly rebalances):**

| Method | Ann. Sharpe | Ann. return | Cum. return | Max drawdown | CVaR (5%, daily) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| EW | 0.349 | 5.67% | 199.6% | 64.4% | -2.49% |
| IVP | 0.082 | 0.76% | 8.8% | 51.7% | -1.58% |
| HRP | 0.057 | 0.54% | 2.5% | 52.8% | -1.60% |
| CLA-GMV | 0.417 | 4.88% | 187.9% | 48.1% | -1.75% |

**Significance (vol-reduction vs. EW, 150-trial circular-shift MCPT):** IVP 6.55 pp/yr, p=0.0067 (the floor at this trial count — 0 of 149 permuted trials matched or exceeded it); HRP 6.42 pp/yr, p=0.0067; CLA-GMV 4.34 pp/yr, p=0.0333.

**Honest read — the first result in this entire project to survive a well-designed null test, but a risk-reduction finding, not an alpha finding, and not a free lunch:**
1. IVP's and HRP's vol-reduction is genuinely real (survives the correlation-destroying null decisively), but comes at an enormous return cost — cumulative return of 8.8%/2.5% vs. EW's 199.6% over the full sample — because both allocators put roughly 75-77% of the portfolio in SHY (the shortest-duration Treasury ETF, the single lowest-variance asset by a wide margin) essentially all the time. This is the correct, expected behavior of a pure risk-minimizer when risk and return are positively related across the universe, as they are here (equities carry both more volatility and most of the long-run return) — not a bug, but it does mean neither scheme is practically useful as a stand-alone allocation on this universe.
2. HRP is not meaningfully differentiating itself from naive IVP here — nearly identical weights (75% vs. 77% SHY), nearly identical performance, identical p-value. HRP's design advantage over inverse-variance is supposed to come from smarter handling of correlated CLUSTERS; with one asset (SHY) such a dramatic variance outlier from the other 13, HRP's own top-level cluster split ends up behaving almost exactly like naive inverse-variance on this specific universe.
3. CLA-GMV is the more balanced, practically interesting result — lower drawdown AND higher Sharpe than EW at a much smaller return cost than IVP/HRP — but two caveats limit trust in it: its own significance is weaker (p=0.0333, would not survive even a simple Bonferroni correction for testing three methods at once, 0.05/3≈0.0167), and the reported number is a blend of CLA's own native solution and the QP fallback above, which triggered on **33% of all real-data rebalances (103 of 309 CLA calls)** — a concrete, real-data confirmation of the exact numerical fragility the HRP literature cites as CLA's core weakness, not just a contrived synthetic edge case.
4. No DSR-style multiple-testing correction has been applied beyond the plain Bonferroni note above — DSR's own machinery assumes a directional-Sharpe search, which this isn't, so a proper analogue was judged out of scope for a first pass and is flagged as a real open item rather than skipped silently.

**Status:** Pillar 3 (Phase 8) is complete — a real-data, statistically-tested capital-allocation study, and this project's first genuinely positive, MC-validated finding, framed honestly as risk-reduction with a real cost rather than a new source of alpha.
